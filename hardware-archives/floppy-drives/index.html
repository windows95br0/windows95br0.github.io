[ COMPONENT RECORD // FLEXIBLE MAGNETIC DISK ]

Floppy
drives.

Floppy drives use a removable flexible magnetic disk in a protective jacket or shell. Personal-computer systems commonly used 5.25-inch and 3.5-inch disks, with different mechanisms, densities, and controllers.

CLASS RECORD: Legacy personal-computer floppy systems and preservation interfaces. External drives may use proprietary host buses; capacities, recording modes, and compatibility depend on model, controller, media, firmware, and format.

Reference specifications

Physical sizes5.25-inch and 3.5-inch are the common PC formats; 8-inch, 3-inch, and proprietary formats exist in other systems.
PC capacitiesRepresentative IBM PC formats include 360 KB (5.25-inch double density), 1.2 MB (5.25-inch high density), 720 KB (3.5-inch double density), and 1.44 MB (3.5-inch high density). Formatting and controller affect results.
InterfaceLegacy internal drives commonly use a 34-pin ribbon cable and dedicated controller; USB floppy devices use internal bridges and may not expose every low-level format.
Mechanical featuresDrive select, motor control, density sensing, write-protect behavior, head alignment, and connector orientation vary by generation/model.

Reading & preservation

  • Identify disk size, density, recording format, write-protect tab, and any nonstandard geometry before attempting access.
  • Inspect for mold, corrosion, damaged shutters, or contaminated surfaces. Do not insert visibly damaged media into a valued drive.
  • Clean and align mechanisms only with a documented service procedure. Misalignment or aggressive cleaning can worsen recoverability.
  • Image disks to a preservation format, keep acquisition logs, and verify the resulting image. Some copy-protected or unusual disks require specialized imaging hardware.
  • Retain original media and document any repair or flux-level capture separately from a conventional filesystem copy.
