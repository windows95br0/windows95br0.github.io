Tripp Lite TLP1208TELTV

Twelve-outlet 120 V surge suppressor with telephone/coaxial protection on the listed model. The specifications below are for this named model and region only; verify the complete product label and matching manual before deployment or service.

ARCHIVAL IDENTIFICATION: Preserve the complete SKU, suffix, region, date code, revision, installed battery part (if present), firmware and physical condition. Similar model names do not establish hardware equivalence.

Specifications, conditions & service

Catalog subcategorySurge protection devices
Topology / power pathMains surge suppressor; no battery, inverter, or automatic voltage correction.
Ratings / output configuration120 V / 15 A-class power strip; manufacturer TLP1208TELTV specification lists 2880 J surge protection. Verify exact revision/label. Twelve NEMA 5-15R outlets; phone and coax line protection connections are provided on the TELTV variant.
Battery / replaceabilityNo battery.
Runtime / limits and conditionsNo runtime; power ceases with the AC source.
Interfaces / monitoringDiagnostic/protection indicators and telephone/coax pass-throughs; no network or energy-monitoring interface.
Safety / installation caveatsRequires grounded outlet. Do not exceed branch circuit or daisy-chain. Telephone/coax connections must be installed as directed; a surge strip cannot assure protection against all lightning or wiring faults.
Model variants / revision checksTLP1208TELTV has line-protection ports and outlet arrangement that differ from TLP1208TEL, TLP1208SAT and TLP1208 models. Match exact suffix.

Field service / archive notes

  • Record labels and connector layout before installation; document load, input conditions, measured behavior and test date separately from manufacturer-rated values.
  • Never open energized mains equipment. Battery devices can retain dangerous voltage or high fault current; follow the manufacturer’s replacement and recycling procedure.
  • Inspect enclosure, cord, plugs, grounding, indicators, alarms and battery condition. Quarantine damaged, swollen, leaking or heat-affected equipment.
  • Network-management appliances should be isolated, updated where supported and configured with non-default credentials; do not expose legacy management interfaces directly to the public internet.
