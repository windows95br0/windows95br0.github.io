DOSSIER M28 // GULF OF MEXICO // 16th–21st century

Bermeja: the island missing from Mexico’s maps

[ MARITIME & GEOGRAPHIC MYSTERIES // MAP ]

Event and chronology

Bermeja appears on some early maps of the Gulf of Mexico, northwest of the Yucatán Peninsula, but its existence was questioned in later surveys. In 2009 a Mexican Senate-backed maritime search reported no island at the mapped location, renewing debate about old chart records and maritime boundaries.

What later accounts allege

The principal allegation is that Bermeja was intentionally removed or submerged to alter the boundary of a potentially valuable offshore area. The geopolitical stakes are real, but they do not prove an island existed in modern times or was destroyed deliberately.

Records, searches and conditions

Historical maps show the name and changing positions, while Mexican hydrographic search reports and modern charts describe the surveyed area as open sea. The map record is evidence of a cartographic tradition, not a geological survey or proof of a recent submergence.

Independent and contrary evidence

The alleged boundary consequences depended on legal and geological questions, not simply whether a label appeared on a map. No reliable satellite imagery, navigation log or modern landfall establishes a recent island; tectonic or volcanic disappearance would leave testable seafloor evidence.

What remains unknown

The origin of the earliest map symbol and the possibility of misidentification remain uncertain. Survey coverage and resolution from earlier centuries were limited, but claims of deliberate state removal require documentary or geological support not supplied by the map discrepancy.

Assessment

Bermeja is not established as a modern island or as a deliberate disappearance. A newly authenticated pre-modern landfall, core samples indicating a recent collapse, or verified records of an island’s removal would change the assessment; map copies alone cannot.
