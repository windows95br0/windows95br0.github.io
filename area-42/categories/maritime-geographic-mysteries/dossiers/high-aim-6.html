DOSSIER M09 // SOUTH PACIFIC // 2003

High Aim 6: the fishing vessel found empty

[ MARITIME & GEOGRAPHIC MYSTERIES // MODERN ]

Event and chronology

The Taiwanese fishing boat High Aim 6 sailed from the Marshall Islands in October 2002 and later ceased contact. In January 2003 it was found drifting near Queensland, without its crew. Australia’s maritime authorities examined the vessel and its voyage, but no one aboard was located.

What later accounts allege

The empty boat is sometimes described as proof of pirate abduction or a secret encounter, with the lack of survivors treated as evidence that official records conceal what happened. A hypothesis about theft has circulated without a demonstrated perpetrator.

Records, searches and conditions

Australian Maritime Safety Authority records and the Queensland investigation establish that the vessel was found and searched; equipment and operational details were examined. Authorities considered possible crew abandonment and piracy, but no evidence identified an attack or established the crew’s destination.

Independent and contrary evidence

Fishing vessels often operate far from immediate assistance, and an empty drifting hull can outlast a crew lost overboard or forced into a raft. The boat itself was not destroyed or removed. No verified ransom, attack report or authenticated claim of responsibility has emerged.

What remains unknown

The crew’s fate, the time they left the boat and the circumstances of any emergency remain unknown. Investigators had limited access to the original voyage and no recovered crew to interview; absence of a trace is not a positive sign of abduction.

Assessment

A disappearance is documented, while piracy and paranormal explanations remain unproven. New crew identifications, forensic evidence of violence, a verified distress message or credible contemporaneous witness testimony could change the assessment; speculation built on silence cannot.
