DOSSIER M40 // BALTIC SEA // 2011 onward

Baltic Sea anomaly: sonar image versus object identification

[ MARITIME & GEOGRAPHIC MYSTERIES // ARCH ]

Event and chronology

A private Swedish diving team reported a circular sonar return on the Baltic seabed in 2011 while searching for wrecks. Follow-up dives documented a rock-like feature and disturbed sediment. Images circulated widely, but the sonar record and site interpretation remained limited.

What later accounts allege

Claims identify the feature as a crashed spacecraft, ancient structure or portal, sometimes citing reports of equipment malfunction as evidence of unusual energy. The specific object identity is asserted more confidently than the survey data allow.

Records, searches and conditions

The expedition’s sonar files, dive photographs and geologists’ comments are primary evidence for an irregular seabed feature. The data are not a complete high-resolution archaeological survey; single-beam sonar can exaggerate outlines and does not reveal composition or function.

Independent and contrary evidence

Geologists have proposed a natural glacial or volcanic rock formation, and the Baltic seabed contains abundant glacial deposits and wreck material. No recovered manufactured component, diagnostic alloy or archaeological context supports a spacecraft or engineered object.

What remains unknown

The feature has not been sampled and mapped with the kind of independent multibeam, sub-bottom and material analysis needed for confident classification. Reports of diver equipment trouble lack controlled measurements and can have ordinary technical causes.

Assessment

The sonar return is real; an alien craft or ancient construction is not demonstrated. Independent geophysical mapping, samples with documented chain of custody and a reproducible material analysis would change the assessment; grainy images and anecdotes cannot.
