DOSSIER M12 // SOUTH ATLANTIC // 2017

ARA San Juan: Argentina’s missing submarine

[ MARITIME & GEOGRAPHIC MYSTERIES // MODERN ]

Event and chronology

The Argentine submarine ARA San Juan reported a battery-related problem while transiting from Ushuaia toward Mar del Plata in November 2017. It then missed scheduled contact. A multinational search followed, and the wreck was found a year later at great depth, hundreds of kilometres from the coast.

What later accounts allege

Claims have alleged an intentional attack, a foreign power’s involvement, or that officials knew the crew’s fate before the search ended. The classified naval context and delays in locating the wreck are sometimes presented as evidence of deliberate concealment.

Records, searches and conditions

Argentine Navy communications, hydroacoustic analysis and the Ocean Infinity discovery documented the final contact, an acoustic event and wreck location. Investigative findings focused on battery, seawater ingress and operational failures; the sound was assessed as consistent with an implosion, not evidence of an explosion attack.

Independent and contrary evidence

The discovery of a wreck at the reported search region and technical evidence of catastrophic implosion undermine claims that the vessel was secretly relocated. The investigation identified serious institutional and maintenance concerns, a documented accountability issue distinct from claims of a foreign attack.

What remains unknown

The exact sequence aboard the submarine and precise initiating fault cannot be reconstructed without full recovery and forensic examination. Public information is incomplete, and families have reasonably criticized authorities; institutional failure is not equivalent to proof of a hostile operation.

Assessment

The loss and serious oversight questions are established; a deliberate attack is not. Full transparent technical records, acoustic data provenance, or a forensic wreck investigation showing externally inflicted damage would materially change the assessment. The current best-supported account is a technical casualty.

Sources and limits

  1. Argentine Congress — Bicameral Commission report, ARA San JuanLegislative inquiry reviews communications, maintenance and command decisions; it is not a forensic wreck recovery.
  2. CONICET — study of the ARA San Juan parliamentary commissionAcademic analysis examines families, officials and investigation process; it does not independently diagnose the technical cause.
  3. Argentine Navy — ARA San Juan search timelineOfficial search record documents communications and discovery; compare institutional statements with commission findings.
