DOSSIER M20 // PACIFIC // 1788 onward

La Pérouse: the expedition that did not return

[ MARITIME & GEOGRAPHIC MYSTERIES // POLAR ]

Event and chronology

Jean-François de Galaup, comte de La Pérouse, left France in 1785 to explore the Pacific. His ships Astrolabe and Boussole were last reliably known in the southwest Pacific in 1788. No return followed. A French expedition later found evidence of wrecks at Vanikoro, tying the disappearance to a likely shipwreck.

What later accounts allege

The loss has inspired stories that the expedition found a forbidden civilization, deliberately disappeared, or was erased by a secret power. Some versions treat years without European news as evidence of a strange geographic boundary.

Records, searches and conditions

French expedition journals, naval records and the Vanikoro archaeological site document the route and later search. Artifacts and wreck remains associated with the expedition support a conventional shipwreck, though identifying individual objects and reconstructing survivors’ fates requires careful provenance.

Independent and contrary evidence

The wreck discovery transformed the case from a wholly lost expedition into a likely maritime disaster. Oral histories and archaeological finds broadly support a loss near Vanikoro, while no evidence shows contact with a supernatural force. Long communication delays were ordinary in the era.

What remains unknown

The precise circumstances of the wrecks, which people survived initial impact and how long survivors remained are not fully established. The archaeological record is fragmentary, and traditional accounts need to be assessed respectfully without treating every detail as a verified eyewitness report.

Assessment

The expedition’s fate is largely explainable as shipwreck, with some human chronology still unknown. A securely provenanced inscription, remains or newly identified wreck material could resolve further details; no evidence currently supports a secret discovery or paranormal disappearance.
