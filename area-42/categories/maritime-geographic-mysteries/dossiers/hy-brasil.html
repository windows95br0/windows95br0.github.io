DOSSIER M32 // NORTH ATLANTIC // medieval–19th century

Hy-Brasil: the island west of Ireland

[ MARITIME & GEOGRAPHIC MYSTERIES // MAP ]

Event and chronology

Hy-Brasil or Brasil appears on medieval and early modern maps as an island west of Ireland. Navigators and writers reported sightings over centuries, but no expedition produced a verified coastline or durable settlement. The island gradually disappeared from authoritative charts as surveys improved.

What later accounts allege

The island is described in modern claims as a hidden land, a portal or a site intentionally concealed from maps. Stories of a mist-shrouded island are often treated as independent confirmations despite shared folklore and unclear coordinates.

Records, searches and conditions

Historical maps and Irish maritime folklore document the enduring image. The map collection shows how position and shape changed over time; reported sightings lack the survey data, independently recorded bearings and physical evidence expected for a real island.

Independent and contrary evidence

Modern hydrographic charts and satellite observations show no island in the reported area. The repeated stories may reflect optical illusions, fog banks, mirages or narrative transmission. A place’s cultural importance does not verify literal supernatural claims.

What remains unknown

It is not possible to identify a single source for every version of the legend, and one historical sighting may have involved an optical event. No evidence demonstrates an island that vanishes and reappears on schedule.

Assessment

Hy-Brasil is best understood as a cartographic and folkloric tradition, not a proven landmass or portal. A reproducible survey locating stable land at a historically supported coordinate would change the geographical assessment; the current record supports no such finding.
