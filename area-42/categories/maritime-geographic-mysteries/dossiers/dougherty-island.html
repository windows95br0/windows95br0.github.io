DOSSIER M29 // SOUTHERN OCEAN // 1841–1904

Dougherty Island: an island reported in the Southern Ocean

[ MARITIME & GEOGRAPHIC MYSTERIES // MAP ]

Event and chronology

Captain James Dougherty reported an island in the South Pacific in 1841, and it appeared on later charts. Subsequent navigators, including the German Antarctic Expedition, searched the reported position and found no land. By the early twentieth century, the feature was treated as nonexistent.

What later accounts allege

The island’s disappearance has been used to argue for secret Antarctic lands or deliberate chart suppression. Some retellings treat successive map editions as separate sightings, even when they copied an earlier report.

Records, searches and conditions

Dougherty’s published account and hydrographic chart histories document the initial report; later expedition logs record searches that failed to find the island. The Southern Ocean’s fog, ice and refraction complicate visual navigation, and nineteenth-century longitude estimates could be wrong.

Independent and contrary evidence

No modern satellite or bathymetric survey finds an island at the reported coordinates. Multiple navigators crossed or surveyed nearby waters without confirming it. An inherited chart mark cannot outweigh repeated direct searches and modern geospatial data.

What remains unknown

Dougherty’s exact original position and what he saw cannot be recovered with confidence. He may have misidentified ice or reported a location error; evidence does not determine which. Failure to find the feature does not establish a hidden landmass elsewhere.

Assessment

Dougherty Island is best classified as a phantom island. A properly georeferenced log documenting landfall or a modern survey showing land at a corrected coordinate would change that view; the current chart and satellite record do not.
