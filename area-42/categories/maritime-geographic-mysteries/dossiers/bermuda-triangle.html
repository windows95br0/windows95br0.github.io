DOSSIER A42-07 // ATLANTIC OCEAN

The Bermuda Triangle

The claim

A loosely bounded part of the western North Atlantic is said to cause a disproportionate number of mysterious ship and aircraft disappearances through paranormal, magnetic, or extraterrestrial forces.

What the record supports

The region has heavy traffic, difficult weather, powerful currents, and ordinary navigation and mechanical hazards. There is no universally accepted official boundary, and famous stories may omit weather, route, vessel condition, or later corrections.

Assessment: The popular claim of a uniquely dangerous or paranormal zone is not established by reliable comparative statistics. A disappearance can remain unresolved without requiring an extraordinary cause.

Useful checks

  • Check original incident reports, dates, vessel identity, weather, route, and whether later evidence changed the story.
  • Compare risk rates using consistent exposure data (traffic, distance, vessel type, period), not a list of dramatic cases.
  • Account for the Gulf Stream, storms, shallow reefs, and ordinary limits of historical search-and-rescue records.

How the legend grew

The Triangle is not a consistently defined statistical region, which makes claims of exceptional disappearance rates difficult to test. Popular lists often combine different decades, vessel types, reporting standards, and levels of documentation; some retellings omit known weather or later corrections. A fair comparison needs a boundary selected before counting, a defined period, reliable incident records, and exposure denominators such as traffic or distance travelled.

Storms, currents, reefs, heavy traffic, mechanical failure, and limits in historical communications are plausible hazards, not proof that every loss has been solved. Conversely, an unexplained disappearance does not demonstrate a paranormal force. The NOAA discussion is a useful summary, while case-level conclusions should be checked against original Coast Guard, maritime, weather, and investigation records where they survive.
