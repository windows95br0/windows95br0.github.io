Intelligence & surveillance

Classified programs, intelligence history, surveillance, experiments and state secrecy.

58 of 58 dossiers are indexed. Each entry evaluates a specific claim, documented plot, or alleged cover-up; unresolved events are not treated as proof.

DOSSIER // 001

CIA ‘Enhanced Interrogation’ Techniques

The CIA used waterboarding and other coercive methods on detainees. Senate investigators found the agency overstated effectiveness; specific intelligence outcomes remain contested and do not resolve the methods’ legality.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 002

CIA Media Relationships and ‘Operation Mockingbird’

The CIA cultivated selected journalists and media institutions during the Cold War. The popular label ‘Operation Mockingbird’ is often used for a far broader domestic propaganda scheme than the public record supports.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 003

CIA Rendition, Detention, and Black Sites

After 2001 the CIA ran secret detention sites and transferred suspects abroad, where some were tortured. Program-level evidence is strong; claims about every detainee or transfer require case-specific proof.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 004

FBI COINTELPRO

COINTELPRO was an FBI counterintelligence program that surveilled, disrupted, and discredited selected organizations. Its own memoranda show intent; they do not prove that the FBI created or controlled every movement it targeted.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 005

Congress for Cultural Freedom

The Congress for Cultural Freedom promoted liberal anti-communism through journals, conferences, and arts programs. Its covert CIA support is established, but not every contributor was a knowing agent or every work an intelligence directive.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 006

ECHELON and the UKUSA Alliance

The Five Eyes alliance shared signals intelligence and intercepted some satellite communications. ‘ECHELON’ is used inconsistently; claims that it read every email and phone call worldwide exceed the public evidence.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 007

Edgewood Arsenal Human Experiments

The Army exposed servicemembers to chemical agents in studies at Edgewood Arsenal. Consent and follow-up were uneven; participation alone does not prove a later illness was caused by a particular exposure.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 008

FBI Carnivore

Carnivore was used at providers for court-authorized investigations. DOJ commissioned a technical review amid privacy concerns, but public records do not establish universal capture or allow full independent auditing.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 009

GCHQ JTRIG Online Influence

Leaked training slides describe JTRIG methods such as impersonation and disruption. They establish capabilities taught and considered, not that British intelligence controlled all platforms or caused every viral campaign.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 010

GCHQ TEMPORA

Leaked records and UK oversight show GCHQ intercepted some transatlantic cable traffic and shared selected material with NSA. They do not show permanent storage or universal decryption of every communication.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 011

Guatemala Sexually Transmitted Infection Experiments

U.S.-sponsored researchers deliberately exposed people in Guatemala to STIs without adequate consent. This differs from Tuskegee, where researchers observed existing infection rather than inoculating participants.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 012

Holmesburg Prison Dermatology Experiments

Dermatologist Albert Kligman and collaborators conducted chemical and medical studies on incarcerated men. The research raises serious consent concerns, but participation in Holmesburg does not prove exposure to every substance later alleged.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 013

CIA Operation HTLINGUAL

HTLINGUAL photographed and examined selected correspondence between the United States and communist countries. Congressional records establish serious postal privacy violations, but not the claim that every domestic letter was routinely opened.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 014

U.S. Human Radiation Experiments and ACHRE

Federal agencies conducted radiation experiments, some without adequate consent. ACHRE confirmed serious abuses but did not find a coordinated program irradiating all citizens; individual illness claims need dose and medical evidence.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 015

MKUltra & mind-control narratives

Because a real secret CIA program conducted unethical human experiments, some narratives extend the record to claim reliably perfected mind control, remote behavioral control, or specific modern events directed by the program.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 016

CIA ‘Acoustic Kitty’

A CIA internal review describes an implanted microphone and transmitter, but concluded the project was impractical. Popular claims of a successful mission or taxi death are not established by that file.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 017

Operation ALSOS

ALSOS followed German scientific research and secured personnel, laboratories, and documents. The mission found no near-complete German bomb program; claims of a hidden Nazi atomic arsenal conflict with technical evidence.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 018

Operation BIG BUZZ

The Army released mosquitoes to study dispersal and survival. Records describe uninfected insects; no credible evidence shows a pathogen release or an outbreak caused by the test.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 019

Operation CHAOS

CHAOS searched for foreign influence in U.S. protest movements and collected information on Americans. The operation found no significant foreign direction of domestic dissent, while its own methods exceeded proper foreign-intelligence boundaries.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 020

Operation GOLD: Berlin Tunnel

The Berlin tunnel tapped Soviet communications, but Soviet officer George Blake had warned Moscow. Intercepts may have been partly exploited or manipulated; neither ‘all useful’ nor ‘all fake’ is demonstrated.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 021

Operation INFEKTION and AIDS Disinformation

Soviet active measures spread the claim that HIV was created by U.S. researchers as a weapon. Archival evidence supports the campaign; genetics and virology contradict the underlying allegation.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 022

Operation IVY BELLS

U.S. agencies installed a listening device on one Soviet military cable. This highly technical operation is documented; it does not show that every ocean cable or encrypted message was accessible.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 023

Operation LAC: Large Area Coverage

Army teams dispersed a chemical tracer over cities to model aerosol movement. This was undisclosed exposure to a potentially hazardous material, not a documented biological attack; population-level health effects remain unresolved.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 024

Operation Mongoose

After the Bay of Pigs, U.S. agencies ran a covert program to undermine Cuba. Mongoose included propaganda, intelligence, and sabotage efforts, but it must not be conflated with unapproved Operation NORTHWOODS.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 025

Operation NORTHWOODS

Joint Chiefs proposed staged incidents that could provide a pretext for military action against Cuba. The declassified memorandum is genuine, but no reliable evidence shows NORTHWOODS was authorized or executed.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 026

Operation PAPERCLIP

The United States recruited German scientists and technicians after the war, sometimes softening or hiding Nazi histories to permit entry. The record documents compromised vetting but does not make every recruit equally culpable.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 027

Operation PBSUCCESS: Guatemala, 1954

The CIA supported the removal of elected president Jacobo Árbenz through propaganda, diplomatic pressure, and an exile force. This covert intervention is documented, but not every later atrocity can be inferred from the coup alone.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 028

Phoenix Program in Vietnam

Phoenix sought to identify the Viet Cong political infrastructure through intelligence and South Vietnamese security operations. It involved coercion and abuses, but reported ‘neutralizations’ included captures and defections as well as deaths.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 029

Operation RUBICON and Crypto AG

CIA and West German intelligence secretly controlled Crypto AG and influenced selected products. The operation is well supported by investigations, but does not prove that all Crypto devices or all customer traffic were compromised.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 030

KGB Operation RYAN

RYAN tasked Soviet stations to watch for signs of a possible U.S.-NATO nuclear attack. The program documents Soviet threat perception and collection—not proof that the feared Western attack was real.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 031

Operation SEA-SPRAY

The Navy sprayed bacterial simulants over San Francisco to study aerosol dispersal without public notice. Test details are documented; later allegations of a proven disease outbreak remain medically unconfirmed.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 032

Operation SHAMROCK

SHAMROCK gave U.S. intelligence access to international telegram traffic from major carriers for decades. The Church Committee documented the arrangement; access, automated selection, human reading, retention, and dissemination were not the same thing.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 033

Operation TPAJAX: Iran, 1953

TPAJAX helped overthrow Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh and restore the Shah’s authority. Foreign covert intervention is established, while domestic political and military actors also shaped the crisis.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 034

Operation WANDERING SOUL

U.S. psychological operations used staged voices and culturally resonant references to the dead in Vietnam. Authentic recordings and military accounts establish the tactic, not claims that it caused mass defections or supernatural events.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 035

Operation WHITECOAT

Conscientious objectors volunteered for controlled exposure studies at Fort Detrick. The record confirms human testing, but does not support blanket claims of secret involuntary infection.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 036

PRISM: Section 702 Collection

PRISM operates under Section 702 and acquires communications associated with foreign targets abroad through U.S. providers. Incidental U.S.-person collection and compliance issues are documented; public records do not show unrestricted access to all…

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 037

Project 112 and Project SHAD

DoD conducted tests at land sites and aboard ships using agents and simulants. Veterans were often not informed; the record does not support claims that every participant was secretly infected with a lethal pathogen.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 038

Project AZORIAN

The Glomar Explorer attempted a secret deep-ocean recovery of the Soviet submarine K-129. The ship recovered a portion before the hull broke apart; claims of total recovery and complete codebooks overstate public evidence.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 039

South Africa’s Project Coast

Project Coast pursued chemical and biological capabilities under apartheid, including toxic substances and covert activity. The program is established, but its operational capacity and attribution of individual deaths remain unevenly documented.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 040

Project FUBELT: Chile, 1970–1973

Declassified files show U.S. efforts to impede Salvador Allende and encourage a coup. Senate investigators found no direct U.S. orchestration of the September 1973 overthrow, which had Chilean command and causes.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 041

NSA Project MINARET

MINARET disseminated NSA reports based on watchlisted selectors, including some U.S. political figures and activists. Congressional oversight documented the practice; later claims often blur watchlist entries, intercepted foreign communications, and…

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 042

Section 215 Bulk Telephone Metadata

The NSA collected domestic call-detail records in bulk under Section 215. Courts later rejected the government’s statutory interpretation; metadata collection should not be confused with recording calls.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 043

Vozrozhdeniye Island Biological Weapons Site

The Soviet Union operated a biological weapons test site on Vozrozhdeniye Island. A 2002 cleanup addressed contaminated anthrax burial sites; present danger claims require current sampling, not Cold War history alone.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 044

U.S. Remote-Viewing Program STAR GATE

Agencies funded experiments in purported remote viewing. Some sessions were judged anomalous, but an independent review found them too vague and inconsistent for reliable intelligence use.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 045

Stasi Zersetzung

The East German Stasi undermined selected dissidents through surveillance, manipulation, isolation, and pressure. Internal directives and files document the strategy, while viral stories about universal poisoning or mind control go beyond them.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 046

STELLARWIND and Warrantless Surveillance

STELLARWIND involved warrantless collection of internet and telephony data under presidential authorization. Internal legal objections and later changes are confirmed, but claims that analysts read every U.S. communication overstate the evidence.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 047

Tuskegee Syphilis Study

The U.S. Public Health Service deceived Black men with syphilis and failed to offer effective treatment. The study observed existing infections; the claim researchers deliberately infected participants is unsupported.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 048

Unit 731 and Japanese Human Experimentation

Unit 731 and related Japanese military units conducted lethal experiments and biological warfare in China. The core crimes are supported by convergent records; victim totals and postwar bargains are less precisely known.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 049

NSA Upstream Collection

Upstream collection acquires some internet communications with provider assistance at network infrastructure under Section 702. Oversight records confirm the method but leave unresolved the scale of incidental collection and filtering.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 050

The VENONA Project

VENONA recovered portions of Soviet communications and confirmed espionage networks. It is not a court verdict for every person named in an analyst’s notes; code names, gaps, and translations require careful evaluation.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 051

Operation Condor

Southern Cone dictatorships coordinated intelligence and cross-border repression. Declassified records and trials document the network; they do not show that every regional crime or U.S. policy was directed by Condor.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 052

Operation Gladio

Italy’s secret Cold War stay-behind network is documented. Its existence and weak oversight do not establish the contested claim that it organized terrorist attacks.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 053

The Rainbow Warrior Bombing

French intelligence agents bombed Greenpeace’s ship in Auckland in 1985, killing Fernando Pereira. The operation was acknowledged and is supported by police and international records.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 054

CIA Support for the 1958 Indonesian Rebellion

U.S. covert support and CIA-linked combat flights aided regional rebels, exposed when pilot Allen Pope was captured. The intervention did not create or control the local revolt.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 055

The Iran-Contra Affair

Investigations documented secret arms transfers to Iran and diversion of proceeds to Contra support. Senior officials’ knowledge and the legal meaning of particular acts remained contested.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 056

The CIA’s Tibetan Covert Program

Declassified records document covert U.S. support for Tibetan resistance and exile activity. They do not show that the CIA created the resistance or controlled Tibetan leaders.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 057

CIA Plots to Assassinate Fidel Castro

The Church Committee documented multiple CIA-connected schemes against Castro. Plans differed in authorization and execution; a discussed method was not necessarily an attempted one.

READ THE DOSSIER →DOSSIER // 058

U.S. Covert Aid to the Afghan Resistance

U.S. aid to Afghan resistance during the Soviet-Afghan war is documented, largely through Pakistan. The evidence does not show direct U.S. creation of al-Qaeda.
