FILE PROJECT/FUBELT // 1970–1973

Project FUBELT: Chile, 1970–1973

Declassified files show U.S. efforts to impede Salvador Allende and encourage a coup. Senate investigators found no direct U.S. orchestration of the September 1973 overthrow, which had Chilean command and causes.

ASSESSMENT

U.S. covert pressure and coup plotting are documented; the 1973 coup was not shown to be CIA-commanded.

EVIDENCE FIRST
UNCERTAINTY STATED

CASE ANALYSIS

What the record supports

CLAIM AND BOUNDARY

Project FUBELT was a CIA effort to impede Salvador Allende’s government and encourage a military coup. Records document covert funding, propaganda, contacts, and coup plotting. The further claim that the CIA commanded the September 1973 coup or directed all Chilean military actions is not established by those records.

CHRONOLOGY AND CONTEXT

After Allende’s 1970 election, President Nixon instructed the CIA to prevent his taking office or destabilize the government. Track II explored a coup before inauguration; Track I used political and economic pressure. Covert support continued. Chilean armed forces overthrew Allende in September 1973, nearly three years after initial plotting.

DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE

The Church Committee, declassified CIA memoranda, and State Department files establish U.S. covert efforts. The CIA’s 2000 report acknowledged support for 1970 coup plotting and later opposition support, while stating it did not instigate the 1973 coup. Chilean and U.S. records reveal distinct domestic military planning.

CONTRARY EVIDENCE AND ALTERNATIVES

Washington worked to undermine Allende, but Chilean military leaders had their own motives and command. The Senate found U.S. conduct contributed to instability but did not find direct orchestration of the final coup. Domestic opposition cannot be dismissed as fabricated simply because U.S. covert action occurred.

UNCERTAINTY

Files remain withheld, and accounts of contacts and intentions conflict. ‘Contributed to,’ ‘supported plotting,’ and ‘ordered the coup’ are different claims. Chilean repression involved Chilean institutions and individual decisions; direct U.S. command for specific abuses requires more than evidence of earlier covert policy.

CALIBRATED ASSESSMENT

Evidence supports substantial U.S. intervention against Allende and coup plotting in 1970, while the final overthrow’s operational chain was Chilean. This distinction neither excuses destabilization nor substitutes a puppet narrative for Chilean history. Allegations about subsequent abuses need separate records.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS

Full contact logs and Chilean military archives could clarify influence over final planning. Explicit orders accepted by coup leaders would alter the assessment. A stronger claim must also explain domestic command structures and the interval between CIA plotting and the coup.
