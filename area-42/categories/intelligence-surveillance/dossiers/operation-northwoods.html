FILE OPERATION/NORTHWOODS // 1962

Operation NORTHWOODS

Joint Chiefs proposed staged incidents that could provide a pretext for military action against Cuba. The declassified memorandum is genuine, but no reliable evidence shows NORTHWOODS was authorized or executed.

ASSESSMENT

An authentic false-pretext proposal; it was not approved or carried out.

EVIDENCE FIRST
UNCERTAINTY STATED

CASE ANALYSIS

What the record supports

CLAIM AND BOUNDARY

The claim is that U.S. military planners proposed fabricating or staging incidents to create a pretext for action against Cuba. A declassified Joint Chiefs memorandum confirms that such options were presented in 1962. The crucial boundary is status: a proposal proves consideration, not approval, implementation, or responsibility for later events.

CHRONOLOGY AND CONTEXT

After the failed Bay of Pigs invasion, officials considered ways to isolate the Castro government. On March 13, 1962, the Joint Chiefs sent Defense Secretary Robert McNamara a memorandum listing possible pretexts, including simulated attacks. President Kennedy did not approve it; the proposal never became an operation.

DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE

The National Archives released the signed memorandum and related Kennedy-era records. The text directly documents extreme deceptive scenarios, including contemplated casualties. Its authenticity is established and researchers can inspect the archival release. It proves what planners proposed, not that an attack later occurred or that the plan was operationalized.

CONTRARY EVIDENCE AND ALTERNATIVES

Contemporaneous records show civilian rejection, and there are no operational traces, victims, or independent corroboration demonstrating implementation. Retellings often quote the memorandum while omitting its unapproved status, then treat it as proof that a later incident was a false flag. That inference is not supported by this document.

UNCERTAINTY

The public record cannot reveal every private conversation, but absence of execution evidence matters alongside the proposal’s status. NORTHWOODS was distinct from Mongoose sabotage and unrelated later events. Similar tactics imagined on paper do not identify the cause or authorship of a different incident.

CALIBRATED ASSESSMENT

NORTHWOODS is evidence that senior officers considered deceptive pretexts, not evidence that one was executed. The document is significant and disturbing without being overread. Any claim connecting it to an actual attack needs independent evidence tied to the specific event, personnel, timing, and operational chain.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS

An authenticated execution order, operational messages, physical evidence, and corroborating testimony would change the finding. A draft alone cannot. Linking the proposal to another event requires evidence of continuity in instructions, means, personnel, and timing while accounting for records showing it was not approved.
