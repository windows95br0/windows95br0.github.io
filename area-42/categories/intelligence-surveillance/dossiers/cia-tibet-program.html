FILE ST CIRCUS // 1950s–1970s

The CIA’s Tibetan Covert Program

Declassified records show CIA political action, propaganda, training, and material support for Tibetan resistance during the Cold War. That support was real but does not mean the agency created the resistance, controlled Tibetan leaders, or directed the Dalai Lama’s religious role.

ASSESSMENT

CIA support for Tibetan resistance is confirmed; claims of total agency control are unsupported.

EVIDENCE FIRST
UNCERTAINTY STATED

CASE ANALYSIS

What the record supports

CLAIM AND BOUNDARY

During the Cold War, the CIA secretly supported Tibetan resistance through political action, propaganda, training, communications, and assistance to exile institutions. The program is documented in U.S. records. It should not be recast as proof that the agency invented Tibetan opposition to Chinese rule or controlled Tibetan religious and political life.

CHRONOLOGY AND CONTEXT

After the Chinese occupation of Tibet and the 1959 uprising, the U.S. expanded covert support to Tibetan resistance groups and exile leaders. A 1968 interagency review assessed the continuing program and its purposes. U.S. support was reduced as Washington pursued rapprochement with Beijing and was largely wound down in the 1970s.

DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE

The State Department’s FRUS edition publishes a declassified “Review of Tibetan Operations” discussing political action, propaganda, and paramilitary activity. A matching CIA review provides operational context, while the Hoover Institution’s ST CIRCUS collection preserves materials and testimony related to the resistance and training program. These sources substantiate CIA support, not every memoir-based operational detail.

CONTRARY EVIDENCE AND ALTERNATIVES

Tibetan resistance had local origins, political aims, and participants independent of the CIA. The agency cooperated with people who retained their own agency and objectives. Evidence that the CIA subsidized or trained some groups does not show that it chose every action or controlled exile leadership. Popular retellings that reduce the movement to a U.S. creation ignore this record.

UNCERTAINTY

Many operational files are incomplete or remain classified, and public accounts vary in their estimates of funding, personnel, and timing. The documentary record is stronger on the existence and categories of assistance than on the detailed execution of every mission or its battlefield effect.

CALIBRATED ASSESSMENT

The CIA ran a substantial covert program supporting Tibetan resistance and exile activity. This is not merely an allegation. But proof of foreign assistance is not proof of foreign authorship or control: claims that the agency engineered the uprising or directed the Dalai Lama’s public role require evidence beyond the existence of the program.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS

Complete budget files, operational directives, Tibetan resistance archives, and corroborated testimony could refine the scale and local influence of CIA activity. Evidence of direct control would need to show that specific leaders took orders and acted under agency direction rather than cooperating tactically.

DOCUMENT TRAIL

Sources & limitations
