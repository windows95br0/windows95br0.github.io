FILE CIA/CASTRO PLOTS // 1960–1965

CIA Plots to Assassinate Fidel Castro

A U.S. Senate investigation documented multiple CIA-connected plots to kill Cuban leader Fidel Castro, including contacts with organized-crime figures and poisoning schemes. The record is substantial, but individual plans differed in approval, feasibility, and execution.

ASSESSMENT

CIA-linked assassination plotting is documented; not every proposed method became an executable operation.

EVIDENCE FIRST
UNCERTAINTY STATED

CASE ANALYSIS

What the record supports

CLAIM AND BOUNDARY

The claim is that CIA personnel or intermediaries pursued schemes to assassinate Fidel Castro during the early 1960s. The Church Committee documented such efforts, including poisoned items and contacts with organized crime. Evidence of planning does not establish that Castro was ever successfully targeted by a given device, or that every plan was approved at the same level.

CHRONOLOGY AND CONTEXT

After the Cuban Revolution and the break in U.S.–Cuban relations, the CIA explored covert action against Castro. Plans included attempts to use poison and approaches through criminal figures. The Senate Select Committee investigated the plots in 1975 after broader revelations about intelligence abuses and issued its interim report on alleged assassination plots later that year.

DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE

The Church Committee report synthesizes internal records, testimony, and interviews concerning multiple schemes. Declassified CIA copies and committee material identify dates, intermediaries, and methods. The report also documents uncertainty about who knew of or approved particular proposals, an essential distinction when assigning responsibility up the chain of command.

CONTRARY EVIDENCE AND ALTERNATIVES

Some proposals were impractical, abandoned, or never reached Castro. A concept discussed by officers is not equivalent to an approved mission or an actual attempt. The existence of assassination plots also does not prove that the CIA caused Castro’s illnesses or every unexplained Cuban event; such claims require independent medical or operational evidence.

UNCERTAINTY

Committee investigators faced incomplete files and inconsistent recollections. The report’s evidentiary base is unusually important but cannot resolve every dispute about senior authorization. “CIA plot” is an umbrella label for different proposals and intermediaries, not proof of a single continuous operation.

CALIBRATED ASSESSMENT

There is strong documentary evidence that CIA-connected personnel plotted to assassinate Castro and explored assistance from organized crime. The public record does not show that all plans were carried out, that Castro was poisoned, or that every method received high-level approval. Each plot should be described at its demonstrated stage.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS

Complete operational files, contemporaneous authorization memoranda, and corroborating testimony could settle who approved and attempted each scheme. Medical records or forensic evidence would be needed to link any specific health episode to a plot rather than infer causation from hostile intent.

DOCUMENT TRAIL

Sources & limitations

  1. U.S. Senate Select Committee, Alleged Assassination Plots Involving Foreign Leaders, S. Rep. 94-465Digital copy of the primary committee investigation using internal records and testimony; it distinguishes schemes and reports gaps in authorization evidence.
  2. CIA Inspector General, Report on Plots to Assassinate Fidel Castro, May 23, 1967Declassified internal review of the plots and the Agency’s response; its account is an official investigation and not an independent audit.
  3. CIA, memorandum on Robert A. Maheu and the Castro plots, June 24, 1966Contemporaneous primary record concerning the criminal intermediaries and poison-pill effort; it documents a plot’s mechanics, not a successful attempt or the complete authorization chain.
