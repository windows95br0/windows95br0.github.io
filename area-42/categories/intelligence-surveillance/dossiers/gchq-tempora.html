FILE GCHQ/TEMPORA // 2008–2013

GCHQ TEMPORA

Leaked records and UK oversight show GCHQ intercepted some transatlantic cable traffic and shared selected material with NSA. They do not show permanent storage or universal decryption of every communication.

ASSESSMENT

Fiber-optic interception and sharing are documented; ‘all data forever’ is not.

EVIDENCE FIRST
UNCERTAINTY STATED

CASE ANALYSIS

What the record supports

CLAIM AND BOUNDARY

TEMPORA was a GCHQ program that intercepted data from transatlantic fiber-optic cables and shared selected material with NSA. Leaked documents and oversight confirm cable access, large-scale processing, and sharing. Claims that every internet communication was permanently stored or readable are not supported by public records.

CHRONOLOGY AND CONTEXT

The program grew with fiber communications and operated by 2011. Snowden disclosures in 2013 described cable access, filtering, storage periods, and NSA sharing. The Intelligence and Security Committee and Investigatory Powers Tribunal later reviewed bulk interception. TEMPORA belonged to a broader legal and technical system that changed over time.

DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE

Leaked GCHQ slides, the ISC report, and tribunal findings document bulk cable interception and sharing. Oversight bodies describe warrants, selectors, and safeguards while acknowledging complexity. Leaked slides refer to large data flows and limited retention for some raw material, not permanent storage of all content.

CONTRARY EVIDENCE AND ALTERNATIVES

GCHQ said collection operated under statutory warrants and filters prioritized relevant material. Civil-liberties groups questioned whether safeguards meaningfully constrained bulk acquisition. Encryption limits readability, and stored data is not the same as analyst review. Slide figures can be misread as people or complete retained messages.

UNCERTAINTY

Technical architecture and complete warrants remain classified. Incidental collection, filtering errors, and analyst access cannot be independently audited from public sources. TEMPORA is not a synonym for all British surveillance, and its historic name does not establish current operations.

CALIBRATED ASSESSMENT

TEMPORA demonstrates substantial international communications interception and intelligence sharing. It does not prove universal permanent storage or decryption. The central unresolved issue is proportionality and effectiveness of safeguards around bulk acquisition; claims must specify capture, retention, review, or dissemination.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS

Unredacted warrants, provider logs, and independent audits could clarify data captured and reviewed. Permanent storage needs retention records; universal reading needs analyst-access logs. One route or period cannot prove global coverage or current practice.
