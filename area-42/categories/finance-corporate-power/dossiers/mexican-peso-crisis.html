FILE FCP-27 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Mexico’s 1994–95 peso crisis and reserve disclosure

ASSESSMENT: Reserve vulnerability and policy missteps are documented; an externally coordinated attack is not.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether the collapse resulted from hidden reserve weakness and domestic policy or an external coordinated plot.

The short-term tesobono liabilities indexed to the dollar made reserve adequacy and rollover risk central to the crisis. Political shocks and U.S. interest-rate changes further altered expectations, while the eventual float turned pressure on the band into a sharp repricing.

Context and timeline

Mexico maintained a managed band amid current-account deficits and political shocks. The December 1994 devaluation accelerated outflows; peso value collapsed, followed by U.S. Treasury and IMF assistance.

The GAO report is particularly useful on what U.S. officials knew about Mexico’s reserves and assistance negotiations. IMF and academic accounts offer macroeconomic explanations, but their retrospective models cannot fully reconstruct investor beliefs in December 1994.

Primary records and formal findings

GAO and IMF records document reserve depletion, policy choices and rescue design. Short-term dollar-linked liabilities and reserve reporting contributed to vulnerability; no official finding establishes a plot to destroy the peso.

Compare announced reserves with liquid foreign assets net of obligations and check what data were publicly available. That test can identify disclosure problems while preserving the distinction between vulnerability, investor selling and coordinated market abuse.

Independent economic evidence

Sachs, Tornell and Velasco analyze reserve adequacy, banking fragility and contagion using cross-country data.

The peg’s reserve and rollover vulnerability can be assessed separately from whether public disclosures were adequate and whether trading crossed into unlawful conduct. These are three different claims.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Political assassinations, U.S. rates and weak banks affected expectations. Rescue terms raise distribution questions but do not prove prior orchestration.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Reconstruct usable reserves net of short-term liabilities; compare announcements with available liquidity and investor information to distinguish misrepresentation from repricing.

Assessment: Reserve vulnerability and policy missteps are documented; an externally coordinated attack is not.

Sources and evidentiary notes

  1. U.S. GAO, Mexico’s Financial Crisis: Origins, Awareness, Assistance (1996)Reviews reserves and U.S. response. Its findings are limited to the parties, conduct, dates or mandate covered by that record.
  2. IMF, Mexico: Recent Economic Developments and the 1994–95 Crisis (1995)Institutional macroeconomic chronology, subject to later criticism.
  3. Jeffrey Sachs, Aaron Tornell and Andrés Velasco, “Financial Crises in Emerging Markets,” Brookings Papers (1996)Independent analysis of fundamentals and contagion. This work tests an economic interpretation; its estimates depend on data and assumptions and do not establish intent.
