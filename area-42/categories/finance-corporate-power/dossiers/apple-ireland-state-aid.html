FILE FCP-71 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Apple, Ireland and the overturned EU state-aid decision

ASSESSMENT: The final EU ruling reinstated selective state-aid finding. Calling this criminal fraud or treating 2020 annulment as final misstates record.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did Irish rulings give Apple unlawful selective tax advantage? State aid is not a criminal tax-fraud prosecution.

Chronology

EC ordered recovery up to €13bn plus interest in 2016. EU General Court annulled in 2020 for insufficient proof. CJEU reversed on Sept. 10, 2024 and reinstated Commission decision; recovery had been escrowed.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

Commission decision and final CJEU judgment C-465/20 P are primary records. General Court’s contrary interim outcome was reversed; final ruling upheld state-aid finding.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: CJEU C-465/20 P (2024) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Final judgment reinstating Commission decision.

Competing account and limits

Apple and Ireland challenged profit attribution; final ruling is not a criminal conviction or proof Apple paid no tax worldwide or conspiracy.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Recovery implementation or litigation on amount could alter consequences; new facts could matter for future tax periods, not erase 2024 judgment.

Skeptical finding

The final EU ruling reinstated selective state-aid finding. Calling this criminal fraud or treating 2020 annulment as final misstates record.
