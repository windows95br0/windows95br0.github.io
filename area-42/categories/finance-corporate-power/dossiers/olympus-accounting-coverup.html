FILE FCP-66 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Olympus hidden investment losses and accounting cover-up

ASSESSMENT: Concealment and named convictions are documented; not every fee or board member is shown to be knowingly fraudulent.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did Olympus hide investment losses through acquisition fees and advisers for years?

Chronology

Losses accumulated after Japan’s bubble. CEO Michael Woodford questioned payments in 2011 and was dismissed. Olympus admitted false reporting; panel reported in Dec. 2011. Tokyo courts convicted former chairman Kikukawa and others in 2013.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

Independent panel, company restatements, FSA action and court proceedings document concealed losses and fee routes. Panel was commissioned by company; court separately determined guilt.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: Japan FSA Olympus action (2012) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Regulatory findings on disclosure and governance.

Competing account and limits

Olympus first defended fees as acquisition costs. Different defendants faced different outcomes; no basis to claim every director had equal knowledge.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Original contracts, bank transfers, minutes and appeal rulings could refine amounts and roles.

Skeptical finding

Concealment and named convictions are documented; not every fee or board member is shown to be knowingly fraudulent.
