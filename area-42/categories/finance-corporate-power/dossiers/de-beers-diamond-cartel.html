FILE FCP-44 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

De Beers and the historical diamond supply system

ASSESSMENT: A powerful historical supply system is documented; current all-market control is not.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether De Beers’ centralized buying and stockpiling controlled rough-diamond supply and prices.

The Central Selling Organisation historically used selected clients, stockpiles and controlled releases to manage rough-diamond supply. Gem stones are heterogeneous, so a single quoted price series cannot capture quality mix or establish a precise monopoly markup.

Context and timeline

De Beers developed a central selling system, buying and stockpiling diamonds and allocating sales through selected clients. The system faced antitrust scrutiny, changing producer-country partnerships and a 2004 U.S. guilty plea over industrial diamonds.

The U.S. industrial-diamond plea concerns a defined product and period, while the historical gem-diamond system involved different products, countries and corporate arrangements. These records should not be merged into one timeless finding about all diamonds.

Primary records and formal findings

DOJ plea and FTC records establish specific price-fixing conduct in industrial diamonds and antitrust restrictions; historical market control over gem diamonds is assessed through company and government archives.

Producer entry and changing sales arrangements reduced De Beers’ share over time. A claim of current control must be tested with present production, inventories and contract data rather than inferred from the company’s earlier dominance.

Independent economic evidence

Spar’s economic history describes cartel management and supply control; production shares and stockpiles allow estimates of influence, though diamond quality heterogeneity complicates price analysis.

Any cartel inference must identify the product market and period. Evidence about industrial diamonds cannot automatically establish the scale of market power in gem stones or present-day supply.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Historical market power is not identical to illegal price fixing in every product. De Beers’ share declined as new producers entered; one guilty plea does not establish a continuing global monopoly.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Compare rough-diamond production, inventory, sales allocations and prices by grade before and after entry; distinguish industrial diamonds from gem markets and voluntary supply management from unlawful agreements.

Assessment: A powerful historical supply system is documented; current all-market control is not.
