FILE FCP-64 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

HSBC AML failures and sanctions violations

ASSESSMENT: Serious institutional control failures and specified sanctions conduct are established; universal criminality of HSBC transactions is unsupported.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did inadequate HSBC controls allow sanctioned transactions and money laundering through its U.S. bank?

Chronology

Senate inquiry reported control weaknesses. In 2012 HSBC admitted sanctions violations and AML failures in a DPA, forfeited $1.9bn and accepted a monitor. Authorities later dismissed charges after compliance.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

Senate report, HSBC statement of facts, DOJ DPA and OCC order detail specific transactions, warnings and control failures. They establish institution-level findings, not every employee’s knowledge.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: DOJ HSBC DPA (2012) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Admissions, agreed facts and monitor terms.

Competing account and limits

DPA is not trial conviction; HSBC cooperated and remediated. Suspicious transfer is not itself proof all funds were illicit.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Transaction files, monitor reports and escalation records could change duration, employee attribution and remediation conclusions.

Skeptical finding

Serious institutional control failures and specified sanctions conduct are established; universal criminality of HSBC transactions is unsupported.
