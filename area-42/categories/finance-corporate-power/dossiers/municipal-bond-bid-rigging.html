FILE FCP-21 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Municipal investment agreements and bid-rigging

ASSESSMENT: Collusion occurred in identified programs; extrapolation to all municipal finance is unwarranted.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether brokers and banks colluded to rig bids for municipal bond proceeds, depriving public bodies of competitive returns.

These cases concerned guaranteed investment contracts for proceeds from tax-exempt municipal bond issues, often arranged through brokers. The alleged mechanism was leakage of competing bids or sham competition, not merely that an issuer accepted a low return.

Context and timeline

Municipal issuers solicited guaranteed investment contract bids for bond proceeds. Investigators found brokers leaked bid information and banks submitted noncompetitive offers; DOJ, SEC and IRS cases and restitution followed.

DOJ pleas and SEC proceedings establish specific conduct, while restitution calculations estimate affected issuers’ losses. The counterfactual bid is inherently reconstructed; distinguish the existence of collusion from how much additional yield a truly independent auction would have produced.

Primary records and formal findings

Criminal pleas and SEC actions establish specific bid-rigging schemes, not that every brokered municipal transaction was fraudulent.

Issuer-by-issuer bid files, broker communications and auction eligibility can test the scheme. GAO’s market-structure report provides context but cannot establish whether a particular municipal transaction was rigged.

Independent economic evidence

Competitive bid spreads can be compared with observed market yields; GAO reviews and restitution calculations estimate issuer losses but depend on counterfactual bids.

A manipulated auction requires evidence that competition was compromised; issuer loss then requires a counterfactual bid. The two findings should not be compressed into one number or generalized across issuers.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Contract structures vary, so a low yield or high fee alone is not proof. Some cases settled without contested trial; settlement scope must be described precisely.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Inspect bid times, quotes, messages and eligibility; reconstruct alternative rates using contemporaneous market data and determine whether issuers received less favorable terms.

Assessment: Collusion occurred in identified programs; extrapolation to all municipal finance is unwarranted.
