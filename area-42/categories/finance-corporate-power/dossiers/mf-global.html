FILE FCP-17 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

MF Global and missing customer segregated funds

ASSESSMENT: Customer-fund deficiencies and controls failures are documented; individual intent requires legal distinctions.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether MF Global improperly used customer-protected funds while meeting liquidity needs after sovereign-debt losses.

Repurchase-to-maturity financing reduced the appearance of ordinary secured borrowing while leaving MF Global exposed to sovereign credit and liquidity risk. As counterparties demanded collateral and confidence fell, the firm’s funding need collided with customer segregation rules.

Context and timeline

MF Global built a large European sovereign-bond position funded through repurchase-to-maturity transactions. Counterparty credit contracted; the firm failed in October 2011 and customer funds were short.

The trustee’s account tracing and CFTC action address different questions: one reconstructs customer property, the other regulatory violations and controls. The occurrence of a shortfall is clear; criminal intent by each officer is not established by that fact alone.

Primary records and formal findings

CFTC action, trustee reports and congressional hearings document segregation deficiencies, transfers and failed controls; settlements and inquiries differ on personal culpability.

Reconstruct daily segregation balances and transfers against customer entitlement, bank wires and approvals. This identifies the point of deficiency and tests whether management knew funds had moved, instead of treating every unexplained transfer as proof of theft.

Independent economic evidence

Trade positions and sovereign spreads show a concentrated, illiquid bet; trustee reconciliations trace where customer cash moved and when shortages appeared.

The customer-property question turns on account-level segregation and transfer timing. Firm insolvency, a rule violation and a particular executive’s criminal intent require separate proof.

Counter-evidence and limitations

The collapse involved leverage, funding dependence and European market stress, not one isolated trade. A shortfall and settlement do not alone prove each executive intended theft.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Trace wires against daily segregation calculations, approvals and liquidity calls; separate authorized movements from prohibited use and test each account’s timing.

Assessment: Customer-fund deficiencies and controls failures are documented; individual intent requires legal distinctions.
