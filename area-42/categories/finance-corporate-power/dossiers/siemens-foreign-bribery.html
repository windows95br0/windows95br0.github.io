FILE FCP-56 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Siemens’ global bribery scheme and compliance failures

ASSESSMENT: Systemic bribery-related accounting/control failures are admitted; blanket guilt for all projects and staff is not supported.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did Siemens employees use slush funds and sham consultants for bribes while keeping false books and lacking controls?

Chronology

After 2006 raids, investigations uncovered undocumented payments. Siemens AG pleaded guilty in the U.S. in 2008 to books-and-records and control offenses; subsidiary pleaded guilty to FCPA violations. U.S./German penalties exceeded $1.6bn.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

DOJ plea facts and SEC resolution contain company admissions on payments, false records and controls; Germany pursued parallel proceedings.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: SEC Siemens FCPA settlement (2008) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Civil accounting/control findings.

Competing account and limits

Resolutions cover defined entities and periods; not every contract or employee was adjudicated. Later compliance reforms do not erase admitted conduct.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Contract, payment and procurement records plus authorization communications could reveal or exclude individual transactions.

Skeptical finding

Systemic bribery-related accounting/control failures are admitted; blanket guilt for all projects and staff is not supported.
