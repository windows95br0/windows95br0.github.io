FILE FCP-10 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Barings Bank and Nick Leeson’s unauthorized positions

ASSESSMENT: Unauthorized positions and supervisory failures are established; a deliberate external attack is unsupported.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether concealed derivatives losses and failed controls, rather than a market plot, caused Barings’ collapse.

Leeson’s dual role in trading and settlement undermined a basic separation of duties. The hidden error account allowed losses to be misclassified as customer activity, while management reports and branch controls failed to trigger timely independent reconciliation.

Context and timeline

Nick Leeson’s Singapore futures operation accumulated Nikkei futures and options losses in a concealed account. The January 1995 Kobe earthquake intensified market moves; Barings failed in February and was sold to ING.

The Kobe earthquake was a market shock, not the origin of Barings’ control weakness. Futures and options exposures were already large; the shock affected prices and margin needs and therefore accelerated a failure that the bank’s own controls should have detected.

Primary records and formal findings

The Bank of England inquiry documents unauthorized trading, false reporting and management failures. It does not find that the earthquake or counterparties deliberately caused the collapse.

The inquiry’s reconstruction permits a test against daily exchange statements and cash calls. The relevant counterfactual is whether proper independent confirmation would have surfaced the position, not whether an unforeseeable event could have been predicted.

Independent economic evidence

Position reconstruction shows how leverage and options produced nonlinear losses. Independent case analyses focus on the mismatch between trading and settlement controls.

The core causal test is whether independent confirmation and separation of trading from settlement would have exposed the hidden position before losses exceeded the bank’s capacity. The earthquake explains acceleration, not the missing controls.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Losses existed before the earthquake, and management architecture enabled concealment. The public inquiry could not establish every trader’s motive or reconstruct all private conduct.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Reconcile daily contracts and independent bank confirmations with the hidden account; determine when a sound settlement control would first have revealed the shortfall.

Assessment: Unauthorized positions and supervisory failures are established; a deliberate external attack is unsupported.
