FILE FCP-70 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Martha Stewart: insider-trading claim versus actual convictions

ASSESSMENT: Claim she was criminally convicted of insider trading is false; she was convicted of process crimes and acquitted of securities fraud.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Was Martha Stewart convicted of insider trading? No: jury convicted process offenses and acquitted her of securities fraud.

Chronology

Stewart sold ImClone shares Dec. 27, 2001 before FDA news. SEC civil insider-trading case settled in 2006 without admission. In 2004 jury convicted her of obstruction, conspiracy and false statements, acquitting on securities fraud; Second Circuit affirmed in 2006.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

Indictment, verdict, appellate opinion and SEC settlement distinguish sale from later statements. The criminal verdict did not convict her of insider trading.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: DOJ sentencing report (2004) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Official criminal proceeding summary.

Competing account and limits

She said sale followed stop-loss instructions; prosecutors disputed that. Acquittal does not prove her account, and civil settlement is not admission.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Broker instructions and trading records could clarify the sale; the criminal acquittal remains essential.

Skeptical finding

Claim she was criminally convicted of insider trading is false; she was convicted of process crimes and acquitted of securities fraud.
