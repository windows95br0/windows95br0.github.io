FILE FCP-58 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Satyam Computer Services’ fabricated accounts

ASSESSMENT: Core falsification is strongly documented; individual guilt should track named court findings.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did founder Ramalinga Raju fabricate cash, revenue and profits over multiple years?

Chronology

After opposition to a Maytas deal in Dec. 2008, Raju admitted inflated cash and profits on Jan. 7, 2009. India appointed new directors; CBI investigated. Hyderabad court convicted Raju and others in 2015; SEC pursued investor claims.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

Raju’s board letter, restatement, CBI case and Indian criminal ruling support fabricated accounts; SEC separately addresses U.S. disclosures. Maytas proposal triggered scrutiny but was not itself fraud.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: SEBI official orders provides a separate record relevant to this case. Indian regulator’s findings and orders.

Competing account and limits

Raju later sought to withdraw his confession. Convictions concern named defendants, not all staff or auditors.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Appeals, bank confirmations, audit workpapers and transaction ledgers could refine amounts and auditor knowledge.

Skeptical finding

Core falsification is strongly documented; individual guilt should track named court findings.
