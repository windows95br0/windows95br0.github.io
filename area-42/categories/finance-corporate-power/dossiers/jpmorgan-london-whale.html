FILE FCP-18 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

JPMorgan’s London Whale synthetic-credit losses

ASSESSMENT: Control and disclosure failures are established; a deliberate plan to rig credit markets is not.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether the CIO concealed escalating synthetic credit risk and misrepresented portfolio purpose and controls.

The CIO portfolio’s stated hedging purpose became difficult to reconcile with its size, changing risk metrics and widening valuation uncertainty. The Senate report reconstructed the trades; supervisory orders separately addressed controls and disclosure obligations.

Context and timeline

JPMorgan’s London Chief Investment Office built large CDX and iTraxx positions in early 2012. Losses escalated as the portfolio became difficult to unwind; the bank disclosed $6.2 billion in May.

A large position in credit indices can affect prices, especially when a dealer attempts to exit into a thin market. Market impact does not itself demonstrate an intent to manipulate; assess trading direction, client effects, valuation marks and internal risk exceptions separately.

Primary records and formal findings

Senate, SEC, CFTC and OCC findings document limit breaches, valuation weaknesses, deficient disclosures and risk controls, not a plan to destabilize markets.

The SEC resolution concerned public disclosure and the bank’s statements. Congressional characterization of the trades is an investigative conclusion, not a substitute for the narrower elements of any court-adjudicated offense.

Independent economic evidence

Trade reconstructions, model changes and index spreads allow independent analysis of exposure and price effects; dealer-market research helps distinguish impact from manipulation.

Trade size, changing model marks and limit overrides support a risk-governance account. Proving manipulation would require evidence of intent to distort a benchmark or harm counterparties beyond losses from a failed hedge.

Counter-evidence and limitations

The CIO called the activity hedging, though size and risk changes undermined the description. SEC settlement addressed disclosures, not every allegation made in congressional rhetoric.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Recompute risk limits using contemporaneous positions and executable prices; trace model-input changes and exceptions to distinguish a failed hedge from false reporting.

Assessment: Control and disclosure failures are established; a deliberate plan to rig credit markets is not.

Sources and evidentiary notes

  2. SEC, JPMorgan Chase settlement order (2013)Establishes specified securities-disclosure violations. Its findings are limited to the parties, conduct, dates or mandate covered by that record.
  3. OCC, JPMorgan Chase enforcement action (2013)Documents prudential risk-management deficiencies. It addresses this episode but cannot alone resolve the broader causal question.
