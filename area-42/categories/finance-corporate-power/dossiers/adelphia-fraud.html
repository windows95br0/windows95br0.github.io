FILE FCP-59 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Adelphia hidden debt and Rigas-family control

ASSESSMENT: John and Timothy Rigas were convicted on specified counts; guilt does not extend automatically to all relatives or employees.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did executives conceal co-borrowed debt and use company funds for private benefit?

Chronology

Adelphia disclosed $2.3bn debt co-borrowed with Rigas entities in March 2002 and filed bankruptcy in June. Federal jury convicted John and Timothy Rigas in 2004; other defendants had different outcomes.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

SEC complaint, bank records and trial established concealment and use of funds; verdicts and appellate review determine named defendants’ liability.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: DOJ Rigas verdict (2004) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Jury outcomes for named defendants.

Competing account and limits

Defendants contested intent and accounting. Acquittals and dismissals limit guilt; bankruptcy alone is not fraud proof.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Appeals, authorization records, board minutes and co-borrower schedules could alter individual knowledge and amounts.

Skeptical finding

John and Timothy Rigas were convicted on specified counts; guilt does not extend automatically to all relatives or employees.
