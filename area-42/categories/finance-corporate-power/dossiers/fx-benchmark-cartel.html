FILE FCP-20 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Foreign-exchange benchmark coordination and chatrooms

ASSESSMENT: Specific collusion and misuse of client information are established, not a centrally rigged global FX market.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether traders at competing banks shared confidential customer orders and coordinated trading around the WM/Reuters fix.

The WM/Reuters fix aggregates orders during a defined window, creating an incentive for dealers with large client flows to manage execution carefully. Sharing confidential orders with rival traders was materially different from ordinary market-making or lawful discussion of public information.

Context and timeline

Investigations examined the 4 p.m. fix and trader chatrooms. Authorities found some traders disclosed client orders and coordinated benchmark-window activity; U.S. and European penalties followed in 2014–19.

The Commission and U.S. orders cover specified chatrooms, currencies, firms and periods; each decision has its own evidentiary and appeal posture. They do not establish that every fix, currency or dealer quote was affected.

Primary records and formal findings

CFTC, U.K. FCA and European Commission findings establish collusive conduct by specified banks and traders, bounded by firms, chatrooms and dates.

Benchmark impact can be tested by comparing execution prices with a counterfactual fix and by isolating independent trades. The legal finding of coordination and an individual client’s measurable loss are related but separate questions.

Independent economic evidence

BIS transaction surveys show FX scale and structure; market-microstructure research explains how concentrated benchmark flows can move a fix, while impact is separate from legal proof.

Evidence of dealer coordination establishes a prohibited process; client-level harm requires a separate benchmark reconstruction. This distinction preserves the documented finding without assuming every transaction was affected.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Ordinary dealer communication is legitimate, and a sharp fix move alone is not collusion. Fines do not prove that every client suffered measured loss or that every currency was affected.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Compare customer orders, messages, execution records and fix windows; estimate counterfactual rates using independent transactions and repeat across dates.

Assessment: Specific collusion and misuse of client information are established, not a centrally rigged global FX market.
