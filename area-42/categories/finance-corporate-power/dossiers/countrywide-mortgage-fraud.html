FILE FCP-63 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Countrywide mortgages, securitization and investor claims

ASSESSMENT: Civil evidence supports misrepresentation in identified securities, not fraud in every mortgage or participation in a single crisis plot.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did Countrywide misrepresent mortgage quality and underwriting controls to MBS purchasers?

Chronology

Countrywide expanded risky lending before 2008 and was acquired by Bank of America in 2008. SEC sued CEO Mozilo in 2009; civil judgments and a $335m SEC settlement in 2011 concerned Countrywide MBS disclosures.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

SEC complaint, court orders, prospectuses and loan reviews detail specific underwriting claims and securities. Loan-level comparisons independently test represented versus actual quality.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: SEC Countrywide MBS settlement (2011) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Resolution on specified offerings.

Competing account and limits

Countrywide argued adequate risk disclosure and unprecedented downturn. Defective loans alone do not prove intentional misrepresentation; civil case is not criminal conviction or sole cause of crisis.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Loan tapes, prospectuses, internal exception reports and executive communications could revise loss and knowledge estimates.

Skeptical finding

Civil evidence supports misrepresentation in identified securities, not fraud in every mortgage or participation in a single crisis plot.

Annotated sources

  1. SEC Mozilo charges (2009)Internal due diligence versus representations.
  2. SEC Countrywide MBS settlement (2011)Resolution on specified offerings.
  3. SEC civil judgment materials (2010)Formal civil disposition against named officers.
