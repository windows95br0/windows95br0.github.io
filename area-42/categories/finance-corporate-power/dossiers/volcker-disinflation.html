FILE FCP-25 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

The Federal Reserve’s 1979–82 disinflation

ASSESSMENT: Intentional anti-inflation tightening and its costs are clear; a covert corporate-control purpose is not demonstrated.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether the Volcker Fed deliberately caused recession to weaken labor for corporate interests, rather than to reduce entrenched inflation.

The 1979 operating shift emphasized reserve targeting and allowed short-term rates to vary sharply. The resulting contraction was an intended consequence of disinflation policy, even though the depth and distribution of recession were not precisely controlled.

Context and timeline

The FOMC changed operating procedures in October 1979. Rates and unemployment rose, two recessions followed, and inflation fell sharply by the early 1980s, with severe costs for borrowers and industrial regions.

Inflation fell alongside unemployment and bankruptcies in rate-sensitive sectors. This is evidence of a costly policy tradeoff; to assert anti-union intent requires contemporaneous records about that purpose, not merely the fact that bargaining power weakened during recession.

Primary records and formal findings

FOMC transcripts document conscious tightening, inflation concerns and debate over recession risk; they show policy choice, not a secret mandate to transfer income to industry.

FOMC transcripts expose disagreement about strategy and sacrifice, while later macroeconomic work estimates how expectations changed. Compare forecasts with realized outcomes, recognizing that counterfactual inflation paths cannot be observed directly.

Independent economic evidence

Macroeconomic studies of expectations and wage-price dynamics find tight policy reduced inflation, while labor and debt outcomes were uneven.

Disinflation policy deliberately accepted recession risk, but that does not establish an anti-labor purpose. FOMC records and distributional series can test stated goals against effects.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Policy was distributive and officials debated social costs. High unemployment alone does not prove the primary purpose was to weaken unions; recovery and policy alternatives remain contested.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Compare forecasts, minutes, wages, profits, unemployment and inflation. Test what officials predicted about distribution and whether a less contractionary option could restore credibility.

Assessment: Intentional anti-inflation tightening and its costs are clear; a covert corporate-control purpose is not demonstrated.

Sources and evidentiary notes

  1. FOMC transcripts and minutes (1979–82)Primary records show objectives, forecasts and dissent. Its findings are limited to the parties, conduct, dates or mandate covered by that record.
  2. Federal Reserve History, “The Great Inflation” and “Volcker Disinflation”Institutional chronology of policy and results. It addresses this episode but cannot alone resolve the broader causal question.
  3. Christina and David Romer, “The Evolution of Economic Understanding,” NBER WP 23846 (2017)Academic analysis of beliefs and stabilization policy. This work tests an economic interpretation; its estimates depend on data and assumptions and do not establish intent.
