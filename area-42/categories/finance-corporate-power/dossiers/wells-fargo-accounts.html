FILE FCP-22 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Wells Fargo unauthorized accounts and sales targets

ASSESSMENT: Unauthorized openings and management-control failures are established; individual knowledge and harm need account-specific evidence.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether aggressive targets caused employees to open deposit and credit accounts without customer authorization.

Regulatory reviews found sales goals and disciplinary systems created pressure to cross-sell, while account-level records distinguished confirmed unauthorized openings from broader flagged counts. The board investigation traced how escalation to senior leadership failed.

Context and timeline

Wells Fargo made cross-selling central to its strategy. Regulators found millions of potentially unauthorized accounts over many years; 2016 enforcement imposed penalties, and later reviews examined executive oversight and warnings.

The CFPB’s order and the Federal Reserve’s later governance action address different harms: consumer account practices and enterprise risk management. Neither resolves every customer’s damages claim or proves each manager knew a particular account was unauthorized.

Primary records and formal findings

CFPB, Los Angeles City Attorney and Federal Reserve actions document unauthorized practices and governance failures. Settlements define institutional liability; each flagged account was not necessarily fraudulent.

Consent artifacts, customer communications and fee reversals can verify harm account by account. Branch comparisons before and after incentive changes can test whether sales targets contributed, while acknowledging concurrent changes in supervision and staffing.

Independent economic evidence

Internal targets, complaints and account records show how incentives could produce repeated misconduct. The independent directors’ report traced escalation failures and leadership’s prioritization of cross-selling.

Account-level authorization records test consumer harm; board and regulator records address incentives and governance. Count revisions reflect verification work and should not be treated as a single settled number of proven fake accounts.

Counter-evidence and limitations

The counts changed as reviews distinguished confirmed unauthorized accounts from suspicious records. Do not conflate employee actions, consumer harm and executive knowledge or treat civil settlements as criminal findings.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Audit account-level consent, fee postings, complaints and escalation timing; compare branches under different incentive regimes and test whether removing targets reduced incidents.
