FILE FCP-26 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Sterling’s 1992 exit from the European Exchange Rate Mechanism

ASSESSMENT: The ERM commitment became unsustainable; one speculator’s secret control is unproven.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether speculators caused Britain’s ERM exit or exposed a peg domestic policy could not sustain.

Britain’s ERM commitment required defending a parity despite recession and rates set partly to support German monetary conditions. On 16 September, the government announced higher rates and intervention, then suspended participation after the market continued to sell sterling.

Context and timeline

Britain entered ERM in 1990 at a rate criticized as inconsistent with recession and inflation. On September 16, 1992, the Bank and Treasury raised rates and intervened; heavy selling forced suspension of membership.

Short selling increased pressure, but a currency attack can be profitable because a peg is vulnerable, not because traders secretly control policy. The state’s reserve capacity, inflation differential and credibility determined whether defense could be sustained.

Primary records and formal findings

Treasury, Bank and parliamentary records establish intervention and policy reversal, but do not prove George Soros or one trader caused the exit. Short selling was legal; government chose the peg.

Official intervention and exchange-rate records measure the defense; macroeconomic studies test the role of fundamentals across several currencies. Neither identifies unlawful conduct by a successful trader in the absence of deception, collusion or rule-breaking.

Independent economic evidence

Eichengreen, Rose and Wyplosz find fundamentals and credibility matter to attacks; sterling exchange, reserve and rate data measure pressure.

Market selling tested a peg that domestic policy could not defend at acceptable cost. A successful currency position is not itself proof of manipulation; reserve and policy records explain the exit.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Speculation accelerated stress but does not explain all peg vulnerability. Conflating successful trading with unlawful manipulation misstates the record.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Examine intervention, reserves, rate differentials and forecasts; prove illegal manipulation only with false statements, collusion or rule violations.

Assessment: The ERM commitment became unsustainable; one speculator’s secret control is unproven.
