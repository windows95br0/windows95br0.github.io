FILE FCP-12 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

WorldCom’s capitalization of network line costs

ASSESSMENT: The specific capitalization fraud is proven; industry-wide coordination is unsupported.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

The question is whether executives improperly reclassified ordinary expenses as capital investments to meet earnings targets.

Line costs were recurring payments to other carriers for network capacity, not long-lived assets controlled by WorldCom. Capitalizing them deferred expenses and artificially raised earnings; internal accounting instructions and unsupported journal entries connect the mechanics to the reporting fraud.

Context and timeline

When telecom demand weakened in 2000–02, WorldCom finance personnel moved billions in network line costs from expenses to capital accounts. Restatement led to bankruptcy in July 2002 and prosecutions.

The bankruptcy examiner and amended filings document the restatement, while the criminal case establishes Ebbers’ liability under the charged evidence. Telecom overcapacity and falling demand explain pressure to meet forecasts but do not excuse the accounting treatment.

Primary records and formal findings

The SEC complaint and bankruptcy examiner document unsupported entries and deficient controls; Bernard Ebbers was convicted of fraud.

Invoice and general-ledger samples can test whether any capitalized cost created a future economic resource. Compare depreciation and cash-flow disclosures before and after restatement to quantify the reported earnings effect without generalizing to all telecom companies.

Independent economic evidence

Examiner analysis compared entries with invoices and accounting rules; reported earnings and cash-flow measures diverged before the restatement.

Operating line costs lacked a qualifying asset basis, while falling telecom demand supplied a motive to conceal performance. Evidence of pressure explains the scheme’s appeal but does not generalize the accounting fraud to all operators.

Counter-evidence and limitations

Overcapacity and falling demand were genuine causes of business stress, not excuses for falsification. The case does not show every auditor or analyst knowingly participated.

Falsifiable checks and assessment

Trace vouchers to physical network assets, authorizations and depreciation; quantify how much reported earnings depended on reclassification.

Assessment: The specific capitalization fraud is proven; industry-wide coordination is unsupported.
