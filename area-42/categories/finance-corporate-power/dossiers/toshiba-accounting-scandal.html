FILE FCP-51 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Toshiba’s profit inflation and accounting scandal

ASSESSMENT: Systematic improper, target-driven accounting is documented; former executives were not criminally convicted, and not every estimate was fraudulent.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did managers pressure units to defer costs and inflate profits, or were disputed figures defensible contract estimates?

Chronology

An independent inquiry examined 2008–14 practices. Toshiba restated results, its CEO resigned and Japan’s FSA fined the company in 2015. Former executives were later acquitted in a criminal trial.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

The 2015 committee estimated ¥151.8bn in improper accounting and described management pressure; restatements and the FSA action establish issuer-level failures. The inquiry was company-commissioned, not judicial.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: Japan FSA administrative penalty (2015) provides a separate record relevant to this case. Regulatory action against issuer.

Competing account and limits

Percentage-of-completion accounting requires estimates; criminal acquittals mean individual executive guilt was not proved beyond reasonable doubt. Acquittal does not undo restatement.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Project forecasts, contracts, instructions and audit workpapers clarifying each manager’s knowledge and the annual amounts.

Skeptical finding

Systematic improper, target-driven accounting is documented; former executives were not criminally convicted, and not every estimate was fraudulent.

Annotated sources

  1. Independent Investigation Committee report (2015)Internal records and interviews; commissioned inquiry.
  2. Japan FSA administrative penalty (2015)Regulatory action against issuer.
  3. SEC Toshiba settlement (2015)U.S. accounting charges and company resolution.
