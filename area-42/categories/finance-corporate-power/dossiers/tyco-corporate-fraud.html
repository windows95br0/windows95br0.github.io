FILE FCP-60 / HISTORICAL CASE FILE

Tyco executive compensation, loans and disclosures

ASSESSMENT: Kozlowski and Swartz were convicted of specified offenses; not every Tyco payment or practice was criminal.

Documented findings, economic interpretation and unresolved claims are assessed separately.

Claim under review

Did former CEO Kozlowski and CFO Swartz obtain undisclosed benefits and conceal them from investors?

Chronology

SEC alleged undisclosed loans in 2002. First state trial ended in mistrial after juror intimidation; second jury convicted Kozlowski and Swartz in 2005. Appellate courts reviewed convictions.

Court, regulatory and primary evidence

SEC complaint, trial loan records and jury verdict concern specific benefits and omissions. The convictions followed a separate retrial, not the mistrial.

Independent cross-checks

Independent corroboration: NY Court of Appeals, People v Kozlowski provides a separate record relevant to this case. Review of conviction and challenges.

Competing account and limits

Defendants argued compensation was authorized and challenged fairness. Other executives were not thereby convicted; acquisitions alone are not proof of fraud.

Evidence that could alter this assessment

Compensation minutes, loan ledgers and appellate rulings could clarify authorization and knowledge.

Skeptical finding

Kozlowski and Swartz were convicted of specified offenses; not every Tyco payment or practice was criminal.
