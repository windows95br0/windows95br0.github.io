DOSSIER A42-04 // 11 SEPTEMBER 2001

September 11 attacks & demolition claims

The claim

Some narratives allege the attacks were orchestrated or enabled by U.S. officials, or that explosives—not aircraft impact and fires—caused the World Trade Center collapses.

What the record supports

The 9/11 Commission documented the al-Qaeda plot and intelligence failures. NIST's engineering investigations analyzed the WTC building collapses and reported no corroborating evidence for controlled demolition. Critiques should address the full engineering record, not only footage that appears counterintuitive.

Assessment: Government failures and later policy decisions are documented subjects for scrutiny. They are not proof of foreknowledge or orchestration. Publicly available evidence does not substantiate the controlled-demolition claim.

Useful checks

  • Separate the questions of who planned the attacks, what agencies knew, how buildings failed, and how governments responded.
  • Check structural claims against engineering analysis, construction details, fire data, and physical evidence.
  • Account for the difference between an official investigation's limitations and affirmative evidence for a rival theory.
  1. National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States — Final ReportCommission report and supporting materials.
  2. NIST — World Trade Center investigation FAQsInvestigation scope, engineering findings, and responses to common questions.
  3. NIST — World Trade Center investigation archiveReports, technical documents, and data from the structural investigation.

Keep separate questions separate

The attacks raise several distinct lines of inquiry: al-Qaeda's planning, intelligence and aviation-security failures, the behavior of individual institutions, the collapse mechanisms of particular buildings, and later policy decisions. Evidence relevant to one question does not automatically answer another. For example, a failure to act on intelligence is not evidence that officials planned an attack; a criticism of one engineering assumption is not affirmative evidence of explosives.

The 9/11 Commission and NIST reports have defined scopes and should be examined alongside underlying records, technical appendices, and qualified independent criticism. A competing demolition claim must account for the full timing, structural damage, fire evidence, and physical observations and provide testable evidence of an explosive system. At present, the public record documents a terrorist plot and grave institutional failures, not government orchestration or controlled demolition.
