FILE TFF-04 // 1962 UNITED STATES / CUBA

Operation Northwoods

A source-led historical case file separates documented events, allegations, and unanswered questions. Uncertainty is not evidence for a particular explanation.

CALIBRATED CONCLUSION: Northwoods is a documented proposal rejected by leadership; claims of its execution are unsupported.

Specific allegation

Claim: Northwoods proves the United States later executed proposed false-flag attacks attributed to Cuba.

Event chronology

In March 1962 the Joint Chiefs proposed deceptive incidents as possible pretexts for intervention in Cuba. Defense Secretary McNamara did not approve them; President Kennedy rejected the proposals, and no listed action is known to have occurred.

Evidentiary record

The declassified memorandum is direct evidence that senior planners contemplated simulated attacks. It labels them as proposals and describes possible attribution methods, not execution.

Independent and contrary evidence

The document establishes intent to propose, not implementation. No operational logs, casualties, or corroborating orders connect it to later attacks; inferring execution from a memorandum is unsound.

Known uncertainty

The file cannot disclose every covert discussion, but every alleged execution needs event-specific evidence. Rejection does not prove unrelated covert actions never happened.

Falsifiable evidence requirements

A signed execution order, matching communications, physical evidence tied to U.S. units, and independent witnesses would establish implementation; these are absent in released records.

Sources and limitations

  1. Joint Chiefs, Justification for U.S. Military Intervention in CubaUsed to document the proposal’s wording and declassified provenance in the primary record. Limitation: it does not settle the absence of event-specific execution evidence and the committee’s different remit.
  2. National Security Archive, Operation Northwoods collectionUsed to compare the chronology and interpretation of the proposal’s wording and declassified provenance. Limitation: conclusions depend on the absence of event-specific execution evidence and the committee’s different remit.
  3. U.S. Senate Church Committee reportUsed as an independent check on the proposal’s wording and declassified provenance. Limitation: it cannot alone establish the absence of event-specific execution evidence and the committee’s different remit.
