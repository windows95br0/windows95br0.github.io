DOSSIER A42-TF-051 // UNITED STATES / CUBA // 1962

Operation Northwoods: A Rejected False-Flag Proposal

A declassified 1962 U.S. military proposal described fabricated or simulated incidents as a possible pretext for action against Cuba. Its existence is documented; it was not an executed operation.

Assessment: A documented Joint Chiefs proposal for deceptive incidents against U.S. targets was not adopted and is not evidence that later attacks were staged.

What is alleged

Some accounts cite Operation Northwoods as proof that the U.S. government staged later domestic attacks. The narrower, supportable question is what the Joint Chiefs proposed, whether senior civilian leaders approved it, and what the document can say about unrelated events.

Chronology

In early 1962, the Joint Chiefs of Staff prepared recommendations for creating a pretext for U.S. military intervention in Cuba. The memorandum suggested staged or simulated incidents, including a false account of a Cuban attack on a U.S. vessel and other deceptive scenarios. It went through military channels but was not approved as policy. President John F. Kennedy rejected the proposal; the administration instead pursued other, separately documented anti-Castro measures. The memorandum became public through declassification decades later.

Evidence

The primary document is unusually direct: it records specific proposals and their intended political use, including manipulating attribution. Declassified records establish that senior military officers formally considered the options and that the paper was transmitted within government. This is strong evidence of a proposed plan, not merely a later allegation.

Alternative explanations and counterevidence

The document is a recommendation, not an operational order or after-action record. Available records show no adoption of Northwoods, and its scenarios were not carried out. It does not establish that a named later attack was connected to the proposal. Similarity between a later event and a hypothetical scenario is not a traceable link; event-specific records, perpetrators, physical evidence, and independent investigations must be assessed separately.

Calibrated conclusion and what could revise it

Northwoods is a genuine example of senior officials proposing deceptive actions that could have killed or endangered people. The public record supports “proposed and rejected,” not “executed.” Citing it as proof that a later terrorist attack was staged is an unsupported leap unless independent evidence connects that attack to identifiable planners and acts. Evidence that could change this assessment would include authenticated implementation orders, contemporaneous operational records, or corroborated testimony showing an order was actually carried out.

  2. Joint Chiefs of Staff, “Justification for U.S. Military Intervention in Cuba” (primary document)The March 1962 memorandum is the central contemporaneous record. Read its recommendation language and routing history rather than treating options as orders.
