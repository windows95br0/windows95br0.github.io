FILE TFF-17 // 1985 ATLANTIC OCEAN

Air India Flight 182 Bombing

CALIBRATED CONCLUSION: The plot and preventable institutional failures are supported; most individuals were not convicted.

Specific allegation

Claim: a transnational extremist plot destroyed Flight 182; inquiry findings document major Canadian security failures, while most accused were acquitted.

Event chronology

A bomb destroyed Flight 182 off Ireland on 23 June, killing 329. Another device exploded at Tokyo’s Narita airport, killing two. Canadian surveillance, prosecutions, and a public inquiry followed.

Evidentiary record

The Canadian inquiry found a terrorist plot linked to Sikh extremists and catastrophic information-sharing and aviation-security failures. Only Reyat was convicted; broader findings are not criminal verdicts.

Independent and contrary evidence

No community should be blamed collectively. Acquittals mean individual guilt was not proven beyond reasonable doubt; they do not erase findings on plot and institutional failure.

Known uncertainty

Some inquiry material could not be tested at criminal trial. The planning chain remains partly unknown; warnings’ reliability and recipients vary.

Falsifiable evidence requirements

Unredacted intelligence logs, surveillance records, and corroborated communications could clarify warnings and roles. Individual allegations require evidence, never identity or association.

Sources and limitations

  1. Canadian Commission of Inquiry, Air India Flight 182Used to document inquiry findings, criminal evidence standards, and victim testimony in the primary record. Limitation: it does not settle non-criminal inquiry findings and incomplete disclosure.
  2. Supreme Court of Canada, R. v. ReyatUsed to compare the chronology and interpretation of inquiry findings, criminal evidence standards, and victim testimony. Limitation: conclusions depend on non-criminal inquiry findings and incomplete disclosure.
  3. Air India 182 Memorial, Dalhousie UniversityUsed as an independent check on inquiry findings, criminal evidence standards, and victim testimony. Limitation: it cannot alone establish non-criminal inquiry findings and incomplete disclosure.
