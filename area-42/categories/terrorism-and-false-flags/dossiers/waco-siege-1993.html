DOSSIER A42-TF-063 // WACO, TEXAS // 1993

The 1993 Waco Siege and the Branch Davidian Fire

A 51-day siege of the Branch Davidian compound near Waco, Texas ended when a fire during the FBI's tear-gas assault killed 76 people. A federal Special Counsel investigation found the Davidians started the fire themselves and found no evidence agents fired into the compound, while separately criticizing the government's initial failure to disclose its use of pyrotechnic tear-gas rounds.

Assessment: The Danforth Special Counsel report (2000) concluded federal agents did not start or spread the fire and did not fire on the Davidians during the final assault, while separately finding the government initially concealed its use of some pyrotechnic tear-gas munitions hours earlier — a real disclosure failure, but distinct from the unproven claim that agents caused or covered up the fatal fire itself.

What is alleged

The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms (ATF) began investigating the Branch Davidians, a religious sect led by David Koresh at the Mount Carmel compound near Waco, Texas, in 1992 over allegations of illegal weapons stockpiling and child abuse. A February 28, 1993 ATF raid to serve warrants triggered a gunfight that killed four ATF agents and six Davidians, beginning a 51-day FBI-led siege that ended on April 19, 1993 when agents used CS tear gas to force an evacuation; a fire then engulfed the compound, killing 76 people including many children and Koresh himself.

Chronology

The ATF raid occurred on February 28, 1993, followed by a standoff during which the FBI took over command; negotiations produced limited releases of some occupants over the following weeks. On April 19, 1993, FBI tactical vehicles inserted CS gas into the buildings using vehicle-mounted booms; within hours a fire broke out and rapidly consumed the wooden structure, with high winds contributing to its spread. Only nine occupants escaped alive.

Evidence

Initial Justice Department and FBI internal reviews in 1993 concluded the Davidians started the fire, citing intercepted internal communications and physical evidence at the scene, but were criticized when 1999 disclosures revealed the FBI had in fact used a small number of military pyrotechnic tear-gas rounds hours before the fire, contradicting earlier public denials; this prompted the appointment of former Senator John Danforth as an independent Special Counsel. His 2000 final report concluded federal agents did not start or spread the fire (attributing ignition to the Davidians, likely in a coordinated suicide/mass-death act) and found no reliable evidence agents fired gunshots into the compound during the final assault, while criticizing the earlier failure to disclose the pyrotechnic rounds as a serious, though not criminal, lapse.

Alternative explanations and counterevidence

Critics, including some survivors, cite forward-looking infrared (FLIR) aerial video from the day of the fire that some independent analysts argued showed flashes consistent with gunfire directed at the building, a claim the Danforth investigation's own retained experts examined and attributed instead to sunlight reflections off debris; this specific technical dispute remains a genuine point of contention between some independent analysts and the official investigation's conclusions, though no physical ballistic evidence of agent gunfire into the compound has been produced.

Calibrated conclusion and what could revise it

The Danforth Report's finding that agents did not start the fire is the applicable federal conclusion following the most rigorous independent review conducted; the separate, factually confirmed disclosure failure regarding pyrotechnic tear-gas rounds is real government misconduct in transparency, but is a distinct claim from “agents caused the fatal fire,” which the investigation did not substantiate. New, independently reproducible forensic analysis of the FLIR footage using original, uncompressed data, or previously unreleased internal communications contradicting the Danforth findings, would be needed to change this assessment.
