DOSSIER A42-TF-062 // SAN BERNARDINO, CALIFORNIA // 2015

The 2015 San Bernardino Attack

A mass shooting at the Inland Regional Center killed 14 people in December 2015. The attackers were killed by police. Investigations documented prior social-media communications and a relationship to extremist propaganda, but no evidence that authorities staged the attack.

Assessment: Investigators found a husband-and-wife attack inspired by jihadist propaganda; the public record shows missed clues, not a staged event or demonstrated government control.

What is alleged

False-flag and foreknowledge claims point to alleged prior FBI contact, deleted online posts, the attackers’ planning, or the fact that authorities did not publicly identify a motive immediately. The credible questions concern what investigators knew before the attack, online radicalization, and whether law enforcement missed warning signs.

Chronology

On 2 December 2015, Syed Rizwan Farook and Tashfeen Malik attacked a county employee gathering at the Inland Regional Center, killing 14 and injuring 22. They later exchanged gunfire with police and were killed. Investigators recovered weapons, explosives, and digital evidence. The FBI reported that Malik had pledged allegiance to ISIS leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi in a private online post on the day of the attack; investigators found no evidence that ISIS directed the operation. The case also became a major public dispute over access to Farook’s encrypted iPhone.

Evidence

Physical evidence, surveillance, victim and witness accounts, and forensic analysis establish the attack and identify the couple as perpetrators. The FBI and congressional review documented their online activity, contacts, preparations, and extremist inspiration. The attackers’ deaths prevented a criminal trial and direct interrogation, but the combined evidence supports a locally planned assault rather than a staged operation.

Alternative explanations and counterevidence

Some communications were inaccessible or deleted; public accounts of the couple’s radicalization evolved as investigators analyzed devices. Allegations that Farook had been an FBI informant or that law enforcement controlled him have not been supported by credible public records. An investigation’s inability to identify warning signs in advance is not proof of foreknowledge. Since no trial occurred, individual motive and the full extent of outside contacts remain less tested than in a prosecution.

Calibrated conclusion and what could revise it

The evidence supports a locally planned attack by Farook and Malik, inspired by jihadist ideology, without demonstrated operational direction from ISIS. Public information does not substantiate government staging or advance operational knowledge. New authenticated communications, informant records, or evidence of specific pre-attack notice to authorities could change the assessment; general online statements or investigative gaps are insufficient.
