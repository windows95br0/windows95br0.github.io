DOSSIER A42-CE-62 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Leaded gasoline: a known neurotoxic hazard

The allegation

Tetraethyl lead was added to gasoline despite poisoning incidents and evidence of exposure. The claim is delayed control of a foreseeable neurotoxic hazard, not a plot to poison populations.

Timeline

Production poisonings occurred in 1924; public-health concerns recurred. EPA phased down lead beginning in the 1970s; U.S. on-road fuel was largely phased out by 1996.

What the record establishes

EPA records document phaseout and rationale. CDC blood-lead monitoring shows population exposure declined as gasoline and other lead sources were reduced.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Lead also came from paint, plumbing, and industry, so falling blood lead cannot be attributed to fuel alone.

Uncertainty and limits

Historical exposure and decision-maker knowledge are unevenly documented. IQ effects are population models, not individual diagnoses.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Lead toxicity, wide exposure, and regulatory phaseout are established. A delayed contested response is evidenced; intentional poisoning is not.

What could change the assessment

Corporate safety archives and causal analyses separating gasoline from other sources could revise the timeline and burden estimate.
