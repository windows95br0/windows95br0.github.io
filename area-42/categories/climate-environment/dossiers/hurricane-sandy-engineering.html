DOSSIER A42-CE-12 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Hurricane Sandy and election-engineering claims

The claim

Claim: Sandy was created or redirected to affect the 2012 election. Timing and unusual track do not establish intent. This file tests the narrow allegation rather than treating secrecy, coincidence, or public controversy as evidence by themselves.

Chronology

Sandy developed in October 2012 and struck New Jersey on 29 October after interacting with a midlatitude trough. Chronology helps separate contemporaneous knowledge from later interpretation; sequence alone cannot establish a causal relationship.

Primary record and data

NOAA tracks, imagery, aircraft observations, and service reviews document evolution; election proximity is not causal evidence. Original records establish what was measured or decided, while independent work is needed to test methods and institutional conclusions.

Independent scientific evidence

A blocking high and trough explain Sandy’s turn; surge, tide, storm size, and coastal development shaped harm. Convergent observations and a plausible mechanism carry more weight than a single image, anecdote, or disputed quotation.

Alternative explanations

Rare track interactions invite hindsight narratives; unusual does not mean artificial. A strong alternative must explain the observations and remain open to disconfirmation; ordinary causes deserve the same scrutiny as extraordinary ones.

Limitations and uncertainty

Weather attribution is constrained by natural variability, incomplete operational logs, and the absence of controlled counterfactuals; these gaps do not turn coincidence into causal evidence. Uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than treated as support for the strongest version of a claim.

Calibrated verdict

No evidence establishes intentional creation or steering; coastal preparedness remains a valid policy question. Documented research, emissions, negligence, or secrecy should not be conflated with a broader unsupported coordinated plot.

Testable evidence requirements

Specify intervention and physical signal; test forecast ensembles and atmospheric observations, not political timing. A persuasive claim specifies what new evidence would change the assessment and allows independent replication, including negative results.
