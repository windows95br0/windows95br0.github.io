DOSSIER A42-CE-58 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Navajo Nation: abandoned uranium mines

The allegation

Cold War mining left hundreds of abandoned sites on or near Navajo Nation. Claim: federal and corporate failure to prevent or remediate hazards; nearby disease cannot all be assigned to uranium.

Timeline

Mining accelerated in the Cold War and continued into the 1980s. A 1979 Church Rock spill released tailings into the Puerco River; cleanup assessments expanded later.

What the record establishes

GAO, EPA, and NRC document abandoned sites, contamination, responsibilities, and the Church Rock incident.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Exposure varies by proximity, water, building materials, and time. Mine counts do not prove disease causation.

Uncertainty and limits

Inventories differ and household exposure data are incomplete. Delayed cleanup is documented; deliberate targeting is not established.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Delayed cleanup and unmanaged sites are a documented institutional failure; evidence does not show intentional targeting of Navajo people.

What could change the assessment

Complete inventories, household water tests, schedules, and tribally governed health studies could refine risk and responsibility.
