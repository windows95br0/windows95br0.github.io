DOSSIER A42-CE-63 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Itai-itai disease: cadmium pollution in Toyama

The allegation

Cadmium from mining and industry contaminated rice fields and water in Japan’s Jinzu basin, contributing to kidney and bone disease. The question is delayed recognition of industrial causation, not a hidden nationwide epidemic.

Timeline

Disease reports arose early last century; research linked cadmium and rice exposure in the 1950s. Government recognized itai-itai disease in 1968; litigation and soil remediation followed.

What the record establishes

Government recognition, court judgments, and monitoring document cadmium pollution and remediation. Clinical and epidemiological research links chronic exposure to renal damage and bone disease.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Severity varied with age, nutrition, dose, and comorbidity. Not everyone consuming local rice developed the syndrome.

Uncertainty and limits

Historical doses and early diagnoses are incomplete; rice concentration does not predict each person’s outcome.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Localized industrial contamination and a disease association are strongly established. Delayed recognition is defensible; assigning all local disease to one source is not.

What could change the assessment

Archived discharge records, dietary histories, soil/rice trends, and follow-up of remediation could refine source and residual-risk estimates.
