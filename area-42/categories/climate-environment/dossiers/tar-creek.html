DOSSIER A42-CE-57 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Tar Creek: abandoned lead and zinc mines

The allegation

Mining left drainage, tailings, and contaminated soil. Cleanup and health protection lagged despite hazards; evidence does not show operators intended every illness.

Timeline

Mining largely ended by 1970; EPA listed the area as Superfund in 1983. Tribal, state, and federal cleanup continues.

What the record establishes

EPA documents drainage, chat piles, exposure routes, and cleanup. DOJ’s 2000 settlement funded work but did not adjudicate every claim.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Lead also comes from housing, dust, diet, and other sources; geology and land use matter.

Uncertainty and limits

Legacy exposure data are sparse. Blood lead alone cannot identify source.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Hazards and delayed remediation are documented; a single plot explaining all illness is unsupported.

What could change the assessment

Property samples, cleanup verification, health follow-up, and operator/regulator records could clarify risks.
