DOSSIER A42-CE-51 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Camp Lejeune: contaminated water and delayed response

The allegation

Marine Corps wells contained solvents from 1953–1985. The concealment question is whether officials delayed action or notice, not whether every veteran illness came from water.

Timeline

Wells closed in 1985; later studies reconstructed earlier exposure. The 2022 Camp Lejeune Justice Act created a claims process long after exposure.

What the record establishes

Navy and ATSDR identify TCE, PCE, vinyl chloride, and benzene in different wells and periods. National Research Council assessed evidence by outcome. Settlement did not decide each claimant’s causation.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Dry cleaners and industrial sites are plausible sources. Sampling is uneven and exposure differs by well and date.

Uncertainty and limits

Dose is modeled; epidemiological association cannot prove individual cause. Sparse records limit knowledge-timeline claims.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Contamination and serious failure to prevent or promptly communicate exposure are established. Individual causation and a coordinated concealment scheme need separate proof.

What could change the assessment

Well records, decision memoranda, validated reconstructions, and independent cohorts could refine exposure and knowledge timelines.

Annotated authoritative sources and source limits

  2. ATSDR Camp LejeuneExposure and health-study background, modeled estimates are uncertain.
