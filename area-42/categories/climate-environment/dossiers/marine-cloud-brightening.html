DOSSIER A42-CE-16 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Marine cloud brightening and alleged secret fleets

The claim

Claim: ships spray sea salt secretly to brighten clouds. Proposals and ordinary ship tracks are not proof of deployed fleets. This file tests the narrow allegation rather than treating secrecy, coincidence, or public controversy as evidence by themselves.

Chronology

Scientists proposed sea-salt tests over marine clouds; field work has faced scrutiny, including a 2024 California test pause. Chronology helps separate contemporaneous knowledge from later interpretation; sequence alone cannot establish a causal relationship.

Primary record and data

Plans, permits, statements, and satellite tracks document proposals; imagery alone cannot identify intentional release. Original records establish what was measured or decided, while independent work is needed to test methods and institutional conclusions.

Independent scientific evidence

Cloud response depends on aerosol size, moisture, and structure; local brightening does not guarantee regional cooling. Convergent observations and a plausible mechanism carry more weight than a single image, anecdote, or disputed quotation.

Alternative explanations

Cloud variability and sensor resolution complicate attribution; commercial exhaust can alter clouds unintentionally. A strong alternative must explain the observations and remain open to disconfirmation; ordinary causes deserve the same scrutiny as extraordinary ones.

Limitations and uncertainty

Research is limited by small test scales, short observations, ecological uncertainties, and governance gaps; these limits do not establish climate-scale deployment. Uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than treated as support for the strongest version of a claim.

Calibrated verdict

Research merits governance; a covert global fleet is unsupported. Documented research, emissions, negligence, or secrecy should not be conflated with a broader unsupported coordinated plot.

Testable evidence requirements

Publish vessel identity, route, aerosol logs, controls, cloud measurements, and replication separating salt from exhaust. A persuasive claim specifies what new evidence would change the assessment and allows independent replication, including negative results.
