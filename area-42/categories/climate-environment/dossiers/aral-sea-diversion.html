DOSSIER A42-CE-66 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Aral Sea: irrigation policy and ecological collapse

The allegation

Diversion of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya for irrigation under Soviet agricultural policy shrank the Aral Sea. The claim is official concealment of consequences; collapse is documented, while deliberate poisoning claims overstate evidence.

Timeline

Large diversions expanded from the 1960s. Sea retreat accelerated by the 1970s; salinity rose, fisheries collapsed, and exposed seabed produced dust. North and South Aral paths diverged after 1991.

What the record establishes

Satellite imagery, hydrology, and World Bank projects document retreat and partial North Aral recovery after the Kok-Aral dam.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Arid climate contributed but does not explain the timing and scale as well as sustained withdrawals. Restoration in the north coexists with decline elsewhere.

Uncertainty and limits

Early water accounts are incomplete; health and dust exposures vary by community and have multiple causes.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Irrigation-driven collapse and livelihood damage are established. Policy failure and limited transparency are supported; a plan to make the region uninhabitable is not.

What could change the assessment

Open basin water accounts, satellite and river-flow data, and longitudinal health studies could revise current-impact estimates.
