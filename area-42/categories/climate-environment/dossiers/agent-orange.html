DOSSIER A42-CE-52 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Agent Orange: wartime spraying and dioxin

The allegation

U.S. forces sprayed Agent Orange in Vietnam. The question is whether authorities understated persistent TCDD risks, not whether every illness is proven.

Timeline

Operation Ranch Hand ran 1962–1971. A 1970 NAS report informed partial suspension; spraying ended in 1971. Site cleanup and veteran reviews came later.

What the record establishes

Mission logs document sorties; studies identify dioxin hotspots at some bases. National Academies rate evidence by outcome. VA presumptions are compensation policy, not individual causation findings.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Exposure varied by route and place. Hotspot data do not imply uniform exposure; other factors confound some health studies.

Uncertainty and limits

Individual doses are rarely recoverable. Evidence for one disease does not establish another; historic sampling is incomplete.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Herbicide use, site contamination, and delayed recognition are documented. A universal hidden cause for postwar illness is not established.

What could change the assessment

Complete spray logs, site sampling, dose reconstruction, and independent cohorts could refine exposure and contemporaneous knowledge.
