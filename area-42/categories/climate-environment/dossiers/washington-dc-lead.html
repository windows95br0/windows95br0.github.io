DOSSIER A42-CE-71 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Washington, D.C.: lead in drinking water

The allegation

A 2000 disinfectant change contributed to lead release from plumbing. Claim: residents were not promptly told the scale of risk and an early blood-lead analysis understated effects. This is distinct from Flint.

Timeline

D.C. switched from chlorine to chloramine in 2000. Elevated lead results appeared in 2001; CDC reanalyzed blood data in 2010 and corrected an earlier analysis.

What the record establishes

EPA Inspector General and CDC document sampling problems and corrected findings. NRC reviewed corrosion control; service lines and plumbing caused highly variable tap levels.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Not every tap was elevated. Lead paint and other sources also contributed; water was an additional pathway.

Uncertainty and limits

Plumbing inventories and blood data are incomplete. Biomarkers cannot identify source without other exposure data.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Corrosion-control failure and flawed early analysis are documented. Claims all residents were poisoned or data knowingly falsified exceed the record.

What could change the assessment

Raw historic records, service-line replacement data, and independent retrospective analysis could refine scope.
