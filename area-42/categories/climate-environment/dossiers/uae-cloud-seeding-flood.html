DOSSIER A42-CE-08 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

UAE cloud seeding and the 2024 floods

The claim

Claim: UAE seeding caused April 2024 floods. The program is real, but event records do not substantiate that attribution. This file tests the narrow allegation rather than treating secrecy, coincidence, or public controversy as evidence by themselves.

Chronology

The UAE has conducted seeding research for decades; extreme regional rain struck the Arabian Peninsula in April 2024. Chronology helps separate contemporaneous knowledge from later interpretation; sequence alone cannot establish a causal relationship.

Primary record and data

Regional observations show a large storm; UAE officials said no seeding occurred, a claim auditable through flight records. Original records establish what was measured or decided, while independent work is needed to test methods and institutional conclusions.

Independent scientific evidence

Seeding cannot create moisture; large systems transport it, while drainage, paving, and exposure shape flood damage. Convergent observations and a plausible mechanism carry more weight than a single image, anecdote, or disputed quotation.

Alternative explanations

Official statements should be checked against raw records; climate attribution studies carry model uncertainty. A strong alternative must explain the observations and remain open to disconfirmation; ordinary causes deserve the same scrutiny as extraordinary ones.

Limitations and uncertainty

Weather attribution is constrained by natural variability, incomplete operational logs, and the absence of controlled counterfactuals; these gaps do not turn coincidence into causal evidence. Uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than treated as support for the strongest version of a claim.

Calibrated verdict

A real program does not show it caused the 2024 disaster; evidence favors regional meteorology and infrastructure. Documented research, emissions, negligence, or secrecy should not be conflated with a broader unsupported coordinated plot.

Testable evidence requirements

Release flight tracks, logs, radar, and gauges; quantify any added water against the full storm moisture budget. A persuasive claim specifies what new evidence would change the assessment and allows independent replication, including negative results.
