DOSSIER A42-CE-73 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Hinkley: chromium-6 groundwater contamination

The allegation

PG&E wastewater contaminated groundwater near Hinkley, California. Claim: releases were inadequately disclosed and managed. Contamination is documented; not every illness or alleged cancer cluster is proven.

Timeline

Chromium-bearing wastewater went to unlined ponds mid-century. PG&E notified state authorities in 1987; monitoring and cleanup have continued.

What the record establishes

California Water Boards map the plume and publish monitoring and cleanup orders. Court records document settlement and later disputes; EPA reviews chromium-6 hazards.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Some groundwater contains natural chromium, so source attribution needs hydrogeology and chemistry. Risk depends on route and dose.

Uncertainty and limits

Plume boundaries change with new wells. A civil settlement is not an epidemiological study or proof of each injury.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Industrial release and plume are established. Environmental misconduct concerns are warranted; every personal illness attribution is not established.

What could change the assessment

Pond records, independent chemical source tracing, full sampling, and long-term health data could change boundaries and causal conclusions.
