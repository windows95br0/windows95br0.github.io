DOSSIER A42-CE-60 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Bangladesh: arsenic in tube-well water

The allegation

Wells meant to reduce microbial disease exposed millions to natural groundwater arsenic. The claim is delayed recognition and poor communication, not deliberate poisoning.

Timeline

Tube wells spread from the 1970s. Widespread arsenic was identified in the 1990s; testing and mitigation expanded afterward.

What the record establishes

BGS/DPHE, WHO, and peer-reviewed work document arsenic and health risks. Geochemistry, not dumping, is the primary source in many aquifers.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Wells reduced microbial disease. Risk varies by aquifer and depth; tested alternatives reduce exposure.

Uncertainty and limits

Early testing was incomplete and some “safe” wells proved unsafe. Disease estimates depend on dose and latency.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Contamination and delayed recognition are established; evidence favors unintended geochemical risk, not a poisoning plot.

What could change the assessment

Repeated well tests, open maps, independent mitigation evaluation, and cohorts could revise estimates.
