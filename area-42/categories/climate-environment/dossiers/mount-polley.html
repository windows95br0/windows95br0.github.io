DOSSIER A42-CE-75 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Mount Polley: tailings dam failure and oversight

The allegation

Mount Polley tailings facility failed Aug. 4, 2014, releasing mine waste into lakes and waterways. Investigators found inadequate design assumptions and foundation conditions; there is no evidence of intentional release.

Timeline

An independent panel reported in 2015. Provincial orders required cleanup and monitoring; operations later resumed under revised approvals.

What the record establishes

Independent panel and Auditor General document failure mechanism and oversight gaps. Provincial water and sediment monitoring track impacts.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Quesnel Lake dilution reduced some downstream concentrations but does not erase local sediment effects. Natural metals and the mine-waste pulse need separation.

Uncertainty and limits

Baseline and long-term biological data are limited. Water compliance at sampled sites does not prove ecological impacts ended.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Failure and design weaknesses are established. A planned dump or permanent poisoning of the entire watershed is unsupported.

What could change the assessment

Long-term fish and benthic studies, independent sediment cores, and updated dam reviews could change recovery and risk conclusions.
