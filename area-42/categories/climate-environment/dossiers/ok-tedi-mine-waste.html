DOSSIER A42-CE-77 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Ok Tedi: mine waste and river impacts

The allegation

Ok Tedi copper mine discharged tailings and waste rock into Papua New Guinea’s river system, causing extensive downstream environmental change. The question is corporate knowledge, impact disclosure, and responsibility, not intentional destruction of communities.

Timeline

Mine began production in 1984. BHP withdrew in 2002 and transferred its share; long-running litigation and compensation agreements followed, with monitoring and closure planning continuing.

What the record establishes

PNG government inquiries, court records, and mine monitoring document waste discharge and downstream sedimentation. BHP’s 2002 withdrawal and trust arrangements are documented but do not settle all impact claims.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Floodplain ecology and livelihoods changed through sediment and metal exposure; attribution varies by reach and species. Natural geochemistry and other land uses matter.

Uncertainty and limits

Long-term baseline was limited and health effects are not uniformly measured. Compensation is not proof that every claim or harm is quantified.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Extensive river impacts and institutional responsibility are documented. Exact health burden and every legal allegation remain uncertain; deliberate poisoning is unsupported.

What could change the assessment

Independent sediment/toxicology sampling, full monitoring archives, and community-led health and livelihood studies could alter impact estimates.
