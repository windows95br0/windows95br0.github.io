DOSSIER A42-CE-68 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Brumadinho: Vale tailings dam collapse

The allegation

Vale’s Córrego do Feijão dam collapsed Jan. 25, 2019, killing 270 and releasing mine waste. Investigation asks whether stability warnings, certification, and oversight were ignored; it was not an intentional mass-casualty plan.

Timeline

Brazilian investigators examined engineering and corporate decisions after the collapse. Criminal cases and civil claims followed; a 2021 state agreement funded compensation and regional remediation.

What the record establishes

Independent expert engineering and official records document instability, monitoring weaknesses, and mudflow. A civil settlement records obligations but is not criminal conviction.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Failure can result from interacting design, foundation, monitoring, and governance problems. Tailings exposure varies by location and analyte.

Uncertainty and limits

Stability assessments are technical and contested; negligence differs from intent. Settlement amount does not measure all individual harm.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Collapse, deaths, and major safety failures are established. Preventable institutional failure is strongly supported; deliberate destruction is not.

What could change the assessment

Original monitoring, safety certifications, court-tested communications, and long-term health and ecological studies could clarify responsibility and residual risk.
