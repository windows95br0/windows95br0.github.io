DOSSIER A42-CE-11 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Hurricane Harvey as an engineered storm

The claim

Claim: geoengineering created Harvey. Studies instead examine extreme rainfall, natural storm dynamics, warming, and human exposure. This file tests the narrow allegation rather than treating secrecy, coincidence, or public controversy as evidence by themselves.

Chronology

Harvey intensified over the Gulf and stalled over Texas in August 2017, producing record rain. Chronology helps separate contemporaneous knowledge from later interpretation; sequence alone cannot establish a causal relationship.

Primary record and data

NOAA reports, gauges, and attribution studies document meteorology; none identifies a storm-creating intervention. Original records establish what was measured or decided, while independent work is needed to test methods and institutional conclusions.

Independent scientific evidence

Warm water, weak steering, moisture, and slow motion explain rain; warming can increase moisture while development raises runoff. Convergent observations and a plausible mechanism carry more weight than a single image, anecdote, or disputed quotation.

Alternative explanations

Attribution estimates probabilities and intensity, not the cause of each cloud; models and rainfall estimates have uncertainty. A strong alternative must explain the observations and remain open to disconfirmation; ordinary causes deserve the same scrutiny as extraordinary ones.

Limitations and uncertainty

Weather attribution is constrained by natural variability, incomplete operational logs, and the absence of controlled counterfactuals; these gaps do not turn coincidence into causal evidence. Uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than treated as support for the strongest version of a claim.

Calibrated verdict

Harvey was a natural storm with climate and exposure influences; deliberate engineering is unsupported. Documented research, emissions, negligence, or secrecy should not be conflated with a broader unsupported coordinated plot.

Testable evidence requirements

Identify operational records and a measurable mechanism; assess climate influence separately with ensembles and observations. A persuasive claim specifies what new evidence would change the assessment and allows independent replication, including negative results.

Sources and source limits

  1. NOAA/NHC, Hurricane Harvey ReportOfficial storm track and measurements, not an attribution study.
  2. Emanuel, Harvey Rainfall Probability, PNASPeer-reviewed model assessment of extreme rainfall.
  3. Risser and Wehner, Harvey Precipitation Attribution, GRLPeer-reviewed attribution estimates warming influence, not intentional creation.
