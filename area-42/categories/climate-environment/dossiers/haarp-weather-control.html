DOSSIER A42-CE-04 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

HAARP and weather-control claims

The claim

Claim: HAARP creates storms, droughts, or hurricanes. The Alaska installation researches the ionosphere, not weather control. This file tests the narrow allegation rather than treating secrecy, coincidence, or public controversy as evidence by themselves.

Chronology

Built in Alaska during the 1990s, HAARP research continued under University of Alaska Fairbanks management from 2015. Chronology helps separate contemporaneous knowledge from later interpretation; sequence alone cannot establish a causal relationship.

Primary record and data

Facility publications concern radio interactions with the upper atmosphere; they report no storm-initiation or steering experiment. Original records establish what was measured or decided, while independent work is needed to test methods and institutional conclusions.

Independent scientific evidence

Tropospheric weather follows heating, moisture, and circulation; no measured coupling links transmissions to rain or cyclone tracks. Convergent observations and a plausible mechanism carry more weight than a single image, anecdote, or disputed quotation.

Alternative explanations

Aurora, radio propagation, and ordinary weather can coincide without causation; online images may misidentify radar or aurora. A strong alternative must explain the observations and remain open to disconfirmation; ordinary causes deserve the same scrutiny as extraordinary ones.

Limitations and uncertainty

Weather attribution is constrained by natural variability, incomplete operational logs, and the absence of controlled counterfactuals; these gaps do not turn coincidence into causal evidence. Uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than treated as support for the strongest version of a claim.

Calibrated verdict

The facility and defense history are documented; weather control is unsupported by physical evidence. Documented research, emissions, negligence, or secrecy should not be conflated with a broader unsupported coordinated plot.

Testable evidence requirements

Specify mechanism and predicted signal; independent atmospheric stations must show repeatable effects absent in control periods. A persuasive claim specifies what new evidence would change the assessment and allows independent replication, including negative results.

Sources and source limits

Sources are selected for the record, not as an appeal to authority. Primary documents establish observations or decisions; independent scholarship tests interpretation. Each source has a defined scope and cannot settle every aspect of a claim alone.
