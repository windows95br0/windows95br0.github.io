DOSSIER A42-CE-24 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Ice-core CO2 lag and greenhouse warming

The claim

Claim: CO2 rose after temperature in ice ages, so it cannot warm climate. Orbital shifts initiated warming and CO2 amplified it. This file tests the narrow allegation rather than treating secrecy, coincidence, or public controversy as evidence by themselves.

Chronology

Antarctic cores preserve glacial cycles; in some deglaciations temperature began rising before CO2. Chronology helps separate contemporaneous knowledge from later interpretation; sequence alone cannot establish a causal relationship.

Primary record and data

Ice bubbles and isotopes record past air and temperature; modern instruments measure rapid CO2 rise from fossil carbon. Original records establish what was measured or decided, while independent work is needed to test methods and institutional conclusions.

Independent scientific evidence

Orbital forcing changed sunlight; ocean CO2 release added greenhouse forcing and amplified global warming. Convergent observations and a plausible mechanism carry more weight than a single image, anecdote, or disputed quotation.

Alternative explanations

Trapped air is younger than surrounding ice; Antarctic temperature differs from global mean and past dynamics differ. A strong alternative must explain the observations and remain open to disconfirmation; ordinary causes deserve the same scrutiny as extraordinary ones.

Limitations and uncertainty

Long records include coverage changes, instrument uncertainty, and methodological choices; independent datasets and explicit uncertainty ranges help bound conclusions. Uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than treated as support for the strongest version of a claim.

Calibrated verdict

The lag is real in context but does not negate greenhouse physics or modern CO2 forcing. Documented research, emissions, negligence, or secrecy should not be conflated with a broader unsupported coordinated plot.

Testable evidence requirements

Align ice/gas chronologies, isotopes, radiative measurements, and carbon budgets; test combined drivers rather than timing alone. A persuasive claim specifies what new evidence would change the assessment and allows independent replication, including negative results.

Sources and source limits
