DOSSIER A42-CE-10 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Katrina and deliberate levee-breach claims

The claim

Claim: officials intentionally opened New Orleans levees to flood neighborhoods. Investigations found failures and negligence, not a planned breach. This file tests the narrow allegation rather than treating secrecy, coincidence, or public controversy as evidence by themselves.

Chronology

Katrina hit Louisiana on 29 August 2005; floodwalls failed under surge and underwent extensive engineering reviews. Chronology helps separate contemporaneous knowledge from later interpretation; sequence alone cannot establish a causal relationship.

Primary record and data

Army Corps IPET and independent studies identified overtopping and instability from breach, soil, and design analyses. Original records establish what was measured or decided, while independent work is needed to test methods and institutional conclusions.

Independent scientific evidence

Surge loading, seepage, subsidence, wetland loss, and fragmented planning explain the catastrophe and unequal exposure. Convergent observations and a plausible mechanism carry more weight than a single image, anecdote, or disputed quotation.

Alternative explanations

Investigations have institutional limits and explanations evolved; design failures do not entail sabotage. A strong alternative must explain the observations and remain open to disconfirmation; ordinary causes deserve the same scrutiny as extraordinary ones.

Limitations and uncertainty

Weather attribution is constrained by natural variability, incomplete operational logs, and the absence of controlled counterfactuals; these gaps do not turn coincidence into causal evidence. Uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than treated as support for the strongest version of a claim.

Calibrated verdict

Severe failures are documented; intentional flooding is not established. Documented research, emissions, negligence, or secrecy should not be conflated with a broader unsupported coordinated plot.

Testable evidence requirements

Require authenticated orders, explosives or cutting evidence, corroborated witnesses, and forensic evidence inconsistent with hydraulic failure. A persuasive claim specifies what new evidence would change the assessment and allows independent replication, including negative results.
