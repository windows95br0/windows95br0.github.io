DOSSIER A42-CE-61 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Cape Fear River: GenX chemical releases

The allegation

Chemours’ Fayetteville Works released GenX and related PFAS into the Cape Fear basin. The concealment question is disclosure and control of industrial releases, not whether GenX equals PFOA or proves every resident is ill.

Timeline

GenX replacement chemistry was used from the 2000s. Concern rose after 2017 sampling; a 2019 NC consent order required controls and water supplies. EPA issued an advisory in 2022.

What the record establishes

State orders and monitoring document releases and controls. EPA advisory is toxicological guidance; peer-reviewed studies found compounds in river water and residents’ blood, evidence of exposure rather than diagnosis.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

PFAS is a diverse chemical group with different persistence and toxicity. Controls and alternative water supplies were implemented; monitoring continues.

Uncertainty and limits

Health evidence for GenX is less mature than for PFOA. Biomonitoring identifies exposure but not an individual disease cause.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Industrial release and exposure pathways are documented; proven widespread illness and equivalence across PFAS exceed compound-specific evidence.

What could change the assessment

Independent air/water monitoring, emissions inventories, repeat biomonitoring, and long-term cohorts could refine risk and disclosure timing.
