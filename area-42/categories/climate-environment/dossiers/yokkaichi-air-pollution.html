DOSSIER A42-CE-64 // CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Yokkaichi asthma and industrial sulfur pollution

The allegation

Industrial complexes in Yokkaichi emitted sulfur oxides and other pollutants associated with respiratory disease. The allegation concerns exposure before effective controls, not every asthma case in the city.

Timeline

Petrochemical expansion accelerated in the 1950s–60s. Residents sued in 1967; a 1972 civil judgment found companies liable, followed by stronger air controls.

What the record establishes

Court and government histories document pollution and the judgment. Air-monitoring and epidemiological studies link affected districts with respiratory harm.

Counterevidence and alternative explanations

Smoking, workplace exposures, and multiple pollutants also affect respiratory disease. Emissions declined after controls.

Uncertainty and limits

Historical monitoring and diagnoses were less complete; the judgment applied to specific plaintiffs and defendants.

Calibrated skeptical verdict

Local industrial pollution and health effects are documented. The evidence supports a specific pollution injury, not a universal explanation for later disease.

What could change the assessment

Archived emissions, long-term cohorts, and comparable districts could refine pollutant-specific effects and control benefits.
