FILE A42-SE-10 // SPACE & EARTH RECORD

The Chelyabinsk meteor was a nuclear strike

Chelyabinsk was a natural asteroid airburst, not a nuclear strike. Video, infrasound, and meteorites independently support that conclusion.

Specific allegation

The 2013 Chelyabinsk blast produced a bright fireball, shock wave, and window damage. Claims call it a nuclear detonation, missile, or deliberate interception. Numerous independent videos, infrasound records, and recovered meteorites provide evidence for a natural asteroid entering the atmosphere.

Chronology and context

On 15 February 2013, an asteroid roughly 20 meters wide entered at high speed and exploded. Dashboard cameras recorded its path and delayed shockwave; infrasound stations detected the event. Meteorites were recovered, including from Lake Chebarkul. Peer-reviewed teams reconstructed trajectory and energy soon afterward.

Observations and physical record

Video tracks show an incoming high-speed object, while recovered chondritic meteorites have natural mineralogy and cosmic exposure histories. Blast damage follows the arrival of the pressure wave. No engineered component or nuclear residue has been verified; multiple independent sensors make a single edited-video explanation untenable.

Established scientific account

A stony asteroid fragmented under atmospheric pressure and released energy as an airburst. The shock wave reached buildings after the light, breaking windows. This matches the trajectory, infrasound, damage, and meteorite composition. A nuclear device would require an incompatible delivery path and diagnostic residues not found.

Limits and contrary evidence

Exact initial size and strength depend on reconstruction, and some fragments are weathered. This uncertainty does not challenge the natural classification. The dense independent records constrain the event much more strongly than online compilations or claims of missing official information.

What could change the assessment

A weapons claim requires chain-of-custody material showing diagnostic artificial components or radionuclide ratios and a trajectory consistent with a device. Natural-airburst models can be tested against video, infrasound, meteorite chemistry, and damage. Existing evidence converges on asteroid entry.

The time ordering is diagnostic: the luminous object crossed the sky first, followed later by the pressure wave that broke windows. Meteorite fragments also preserve a natural chondritic composition. A weapons explanation must account for those records together, rather than select a dramatic blast clip while ignoring recovered material and independent timing.

Calibrated conclusion

Chelyabinsk was a natural asteroid airburst, not a nuclear strike. Video, infrasound, and meteorites independently support that conclusion.
