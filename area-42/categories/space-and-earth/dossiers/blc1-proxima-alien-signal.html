FILE A42-SE-055 // SPACE & EARTH RECORD

Breakthrough Listen’s BLC1 as a confirmed Proxima Centauri message

BLC1 was a real SETI candidate signal but is best explained as terrestrial radio interference. It is not evidence of a message from Proxima Centauri or an established discovery hidden from the public.

Specific allegation

The specific claim is that the narrowband candidate BLC1, detected during a Breakthrough Listen observation toward Proxima Centauri in 2019, was an alien signal that was later suppressed. The candidate merited analysis because its frequency drift resembled a celestial source. Subsequent observations, signal clustering, and interference tests provide a falsifiable alternative: local radio-frequency interference.

Scientific and historical chronology

Breakthrough Listen observed Proxima Centauri with Parkes/Murriyang in April and May 2019. A narrowband signal appeared in on-source scans but not immediately adjacent off-source scans, prompting a long technical investigation. The team later reported that many related signals appeared across observations and that their frequency behavior matched a terrestrial interference family. The work was published in 2021.

Observations and records

BLC1 was a very narrow signal near 982 MHz, not a decoded message. It drifted in frequency, initially resembling a Doppler-shifted celestial source, but the same signal class appeared at multiple frequencies and times. The telescope’s beam and on/off scans were useful but not conclusive alone; interference can enter through side lobes or vary with time. No independent observatory confirmed a Proxima-origin signal.

Mainstream explanation and counterevidence

The published analysis attributes BLC1 to human-made interference, likely associated with oscillators or electronic equipment, based on its relation to other signals and failure to recur as a sky-fixed source. This is a successful candidate-vetting example: an interesting signal was investigated and reported, then rejected after tests.

Uncertainties and limits

The exact electronic source may not have been pinpointed, and radio interference is often difficult to localize. The signal’s original on-source appearance was not fabricated; it was simply insufficient to establish an extraterrestrial origin. Public scientific papers describe the investigation, inconsistent with a concealed confirmed message.

Evidence that could change the assessment

A new narrowband signal at the same sky coordinates, independently detected at geographically separated observatories, with celestial Doppler drift and no matching local interference in control scans, would reopen the question. Releasing instrument logs and repeating the observing protocol are stronger tests than reinterpreting BLC1 alone.

Calibrated verdict

BLC1 was a real SETI candidate signal but is best explained as terrestrial radio interference. It is not evidence of a message from Proxima Centauri or an established discovery hidden from the public.
