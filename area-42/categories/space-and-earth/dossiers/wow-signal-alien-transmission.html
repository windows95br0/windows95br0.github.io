FILE A42-SE-052 // SPACE & EARTH RECORD

The 1977 Wow! signal as a confirmed alien transmission

The Wow! signal is an unresolved historical radio event, not a confirmed alien message. No credible evidence shows that its origin was established and concealed.

Specific allegation

The specific allegation is that the 72-second “Wow!” radio event detected by Ohio State’s Big Ear telescope was a deliberate message from extraterrestrial intelligence and that astronomers subsequently hid proof. The original printout supports a strong, narrowband-like transient in one observing beam; it does not encode a decoded message or identify a transmitter. Repeatability and independent localization are essential tests.

Scientific and historical chronology

Big Ear recorded the event on 15 August 1977 during a sky survey. Astronomer Jerry Ehman circled the sequence “6EQUJ5” on a computer printout and wrote “Wow!” in the margin. Follow-up searches did not reproduce the signal. Later re-observations and searches have produced no independently confirmed repetition at the same coordinates. The event became famous through public discussion, not a secret mission finding.

Observations and records

The telescope’s two feed horns scanned the region as Earth rotated; the signal’s rise and fall fit a source crossing a beam, but only one horn recorded it. The printed characters encode signal-to-noise levels across successive samples, not a language. No frequency drift, modulation pattern, or second telescope detection established an artificial source. The archival observation is real but sparse.

Mainstream explanation and counterevidence

A terrestrial or satellite interference event, an unusual natural radio transient, or an instrumental/data artifact remain possible explanations. The event’s apparent narrowband character made it interesting to SETI researchers, but radio interference can also be narrowband. A genuine extraterrestrial hypothesis remains unverified because the event has not recurred and there is no independent position or signal content.

Uncertainties and limits

The original receiver was single-channel and the old records lack the bandwidth and calibration metadata modern surveys would preserve. The source could have been intermittent or below later search sensitivity. These limitations mean its origin remains unknown; they do not privilege aliens over mundane alternatives, and there is no evidence of a decoded message or withheld confirmation.

Evidence that could change the assessment

A repeat signal with matching sky position, a measurable celestial Doppler drift, a coherent structure inconsistent with known interference, and simultaneous detection by independent observatories would materially change the assessment. Release of original instrument logs or a verified sender signature would also matter. A single unrepeatable event cannot meet that standard.

Calibrated verdict

The Wow! signal is an unresolved historical radio event, not a confirmed alien message. No credible evidence shows that its origin was established and concealed.
