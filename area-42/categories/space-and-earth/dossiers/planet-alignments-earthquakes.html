Planetary alignments trigger major earthquakes

Planetary alignments do not provide a demonstrated trigger or prediction method for major earthquakes. Weak tidal modulation is much narrower than the claim.

Specific allegation

The claim says planetary alignments or the Moon’s position trigger major earthquakes, sometimes predicting disasters on specific dates. Lunar and solar tides can slightly modulate stress in limited settings. That narrow effect is often expanded into a planetary trigger, for which no reliable global prediction signal has been established.

Chronology and context

Researchers have compared earthquake catalogs with lunar phases, tides, and planetary positions for decades. Some studies report weak modulation in particular low-frequency earthquake classes. Major destructive earthquakes do not follow a consistent alignment pattern, and broad public predictions have repeatedly failed prospective checks.

Observations and physical record

Earthquake catalogs include many events with locations and magnitudes; JPL ephemerides calculate planetary positions. Analyses do not show a repeatable global correlation sufficient for forecasting large quakes. Statistical associations can appear after selecting dates, regions, or thresholds from a large dataset.

Established scientific account

Moon and Sun tidal stresses are small relative to tectonic loading, though they may slightly affect faults already near failure. Planets exert far weaker effects. A subtle influence on a specialized event class would not determine whether or when a major earthquake occurs.

Limits and contrary evidence

Tidal modulation in some circumstances remains a legitimate research topic. Faults and catalogs vary, and mechanisms are complex. These caveats do not support reliable alignment-based warnings; post-hoc correlations need prospective replication and a physical model.

What could change the assessment

Preregister alignment criteria, magnitude, region, and time window, then compare forecasts against baseline rates over many years. Account for catalog completeness and false positives. Narrow tidal effects should be studied separately from broad claims of planetary disaster prediction.

Earthquakes release stress accumulated on faults; planetary positions do not provide a reliable calendar for that process. A weak tidal influence under special conditions should be reported with its effect size and limits. A broad warning system would need demonstrated prospective skill beyond ordinary earthquake-rate baselines.

Calibrated conclusion

Planetary alignments do not provide a demonstrated trigger or prediction method for major earthquakes. Weak tidal modulation is much narrower than the claim.
