FILE A42-SE-069 // SPACE & EARTH RECORD

The Kola Superdeep Borehole recorded voices from hell

The borehole and its extreme temperatures are real; the “voices from hell” story is unsupported and its audio has no authenticated connection to Kola. The project ended for ordinary technical and funding reasons.

Specific allegation

The specific claim is that Soviet scientists lowered a microphone into the Kola Superdeep Borehole and recorded the screams of people in a subterranean “hell,” then sealed the site. The claim is falsifiable against the project’s drilling logs, physical limits, and the provenance of the circulated audio. No authenticated project record contains such a recording.

Scientific and historical chronology

The Soviet Kola project began in 1970 as a scientific deep-drilling program and reached 12,262 meters in 1989. Drilling slowed and ended in the early 1990s amid technical difficulties, rising temperatures, and funding changes. The “Well to Hell” story circulated in religious and tabloid media in the late 1980s and 1990s, with no verifiable chain of custody to the borehole team.

Observations and records

Scientific reports describe core samples, rock layers, fluids, and bottom-hole temperatures approaching 180°C—conditions that challenged drilling equipment. The borehole is only centimeters wide at depth, not a cavern. Published geological records contain no microphone experiment or anomalous acoustic event. The famous audio has no original recording, instrument log, date, or identified engineer.

Mainstream explanation and counterevidence

Kola stopped as a difficult scientific engineering project in hot, hard rock, not because it penetrated a supernatural realm. The circulated sound is commonly traced to recycled sound effects and an unverified story. The measurable borehole record is consistent with geology and engineering limits.

Uncertainties and limits

Some project documentation is not easily accessible in English, and national archives can be incomplete. That does not authenticate a recording without provenance. At great depth, drill noise and fluid movement would produce complex signals; interpreting them as voices would require calibrated instruments and independent analysis.

Evidence that could change the assessment

A contemporaneous, original audio tape with verified borehole instrument metadata, independent witnesses, a chain of custody, and reproducible signal analysis would require reassessment. Authenticated drilling records should establish the microphone’s existence and operating conditions; none has been produced for the alleged recording.

Calibrated verdict

The borehole and its extreme temperatures are real; the “voices from hell” story is unsupported and its audio has no authenticated connection to Kola. The project ended for ordinary technical and funding reasons.
