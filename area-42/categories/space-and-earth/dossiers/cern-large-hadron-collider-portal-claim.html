CERN’s Large Hadron Collider Opens Portals to Other Dimensions

CERN's Large Hadron Collider (LHC) has never opened a portal to another dimension, and no credible physical mechanism supports that it could; the claim rests on a viral 2016 hoax video, misreadings of routine safety studies about microscopic (and harmless) hypothetical black holes, and the presence of an unrelated donated Shiva statue on CERN's campus.

Specific allegation

The Large Hadron Collider is a 27-kilometer particle accelerator operated by CERN near Geneva, Switzerland, colliding protons and heavy ions at high energies to study fundamental physics, most famously confirming the Higgs boson in 2012. Since it began operating in 2008, recurring viral claims allege the LHC opens “portals” to other dimensions, parallel timelines, or even “hell,” sometimes tied to the collective false-memory phenomenon known as the Mandela Effect, and to a bronze statue of the Hindu deity Shiva Nataraja on CERN's grounds.

Scientific and historical chronology

The Shiva statue was a gift from the Government of India in 2004, marking a long-standing collaboration between CERN and Indian scientific institutions; the sculpture symbolizes the cosmic dance of creation and destruction, a metaphor Indian physicists have long used for particle physics, and predates the LHC's completion. In 2016, a video circulated online purporting to show a staged ritual sacrifice near the statue on CERN grounds; CERN publicly confirmed the video was an unauthorized prank filmed by individuals not associated with its research staff and that no one was harmed, and it violated the facility's rules.

Observations and records

Physicists both inside and outside CERN, along with independent safety review panels commissioned before the LHC's initial startup, have repeatedly confirmed that its proton collisions occur at energies significantly lower than collisions routinely produced when cosmic rays strike Earth's atmosphere and other astronomical bodies, a natural process that has occurred continuously for billions of years without producing any observed catastrophic effect; this is the core physical argument against black-hole or portal danger claims.

Mainstream explanation and counterevidence

Independent fact-checking organizations (including AFP Fact Check) and physicists have specifically addressed viral claims that the LHC could create planet-destroying black holes, explaining that any microscopic black holes theoretically produced in such collisions (a possibility only under certain speculative extra-dimensional models, not the Standard Model) would, per established physics, evaporate via Hawking radiation almost instantaneously and pose no macroscopic danger, consistent with the absence of any observed effect from decades of natural higher-energy cosmic-ray collisions.

Uncertainties and limits

The “Mandela Effect” — widely shared but demonstrably false collective memories, such as misremembering the spelling of a brand name or a cartoon character's clothing color — is a well-studied psychological phenomenon rooted in memory reconstruction errors and social conformity, unrelated to any physical experiment; no peer-reviewed research links LHC operation timing to any measurable shift in collective memory patterns, and the claimed correlations are coincidental or invented after the fact.

Evidence that could change the assessment

Additional claims sometimes cite the LHC's proximity to CERN's founding acronym or numerological reinterpretations of “CERN” as evidence of occult intent; these are wordplay-based assertions with no physical evidentiary basis and are not addressed by mainstream physics because they make no falsifiable prediction to test.

Calibrated verdict

CERN's Large Hadron Collider (LHC) has never opened a portal to another dimension, and no credible physical mechanism supports that it could; the claim rests on a viral 2016 hoax video, misreadings of routine safety studies about microscopic (and harmless) hypothetical black holes, and the presence of an unrelated donated Shiva statue on CERN's campus.
