FILE A42-SE-10 // SPACE & EARTH RECORD

The Event Horizon Telescope fabricated the black-hole image

The M87 image is a computational radio image based on real telescope data, not an ordinary snapshot. Its limitations are public; fabrication claims lack supporting evidence.

Specific allegation

After the Event Horizon Telescope released the first M87 black-hole image in 2019, some said the orange ring was CGI presented as data. It is a computational reconstruction from radio interferometry, not a conventional photograph—a distinction stated publicly. The issue is whether independent pipelines robustly recover the ring from telescope measurements.

Chronology and context

Eight observatories recorded synchronized radio signals in April 2017. Teams calibrated the measurements and used multiple image-reconstruction methods, including blind tests with simulated data. Peer-reviewed papers appeared in 2019. Later EHT observations added polarization and variability measurements, providing further checks beyond a single image.

Observations and physical record

Very-long-baseline interferometry combines signals from separated telescopes into an Earth-sized virtual aperture. Sparse data require algorithms, so color and contrast are choices; underlying visibility measurements are recorded. Independent teams recovered ring morphology consistent with hot plasma around a black hole, and later datasets show related structure.

Established scientific account

The image is not a direct photograph of the event horizon itself. It visualizes radio emission around the black hole, with uncertainties from calibration and reconstruction. Multiple tests and algorithms address those uncertainties. The image’s orange color is a display palette, not the object’s visible-light appearance.

Limits and contrary evidence

Imaging is technically complex and the precise reconstruction is not unique in every detail. Scrutiny is warranted, but complexity does not imply fabrication. Independent telescopes, validation tests, published methods, and later observations provide evidence against an invented image. No credible raw-data discrepancy has been shown.

What could change the assessment

Identify a falsifiable inconsistency in visibility data or calibration, then reanalyze public observations independently. More telescopes, frequencies, and time-resolved observations can refine the image. Color choices alone are not evidence of fabrication; a genuine methodological flaw would be testable and publishable.

The orange ring is a false-color visualization of radio brightness, not an optical photograph or an unprocessed camera frame. That fact should be disclosed, but it is not a defect unique to this result: radio interferometry routinely reconstructs images from calibrated visibilities. Later polarization data provide a distinct check on the emission geometry.

Calibrated conclusion

The M87 image is a computational radio image based on real telescope data, not an ordinary snapshot. Its limitations are public; fabrication claims lack supporting evidence.
