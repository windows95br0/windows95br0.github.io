UAP-057 // CASE REVIEW

“GoFast” Navy infrared video (2015)

Parallax accounts for much of the apparent speed; exact identity is unresolved, not exotic by default.

The precise claim

A Navy infrared clip appears to show a small object racing just above the ocean; popular readings take the parallax effect and apparent speed as evidence of extraordinary flight.

Chronology and provenance

An F/A-18 recorded the clip in 2015 off Florida; DoD authenticated the video in 2020. The release includes sensor display information but not every underlying aircraft and sensor parameter. AARO later published a case-resolution analysis of the apparent motion.

Public records and observations

The original public Navy clip and DoD confirmation are primary evidence that a sensor recorded the image. AARO’s later technical analysis estimates the object’s motion relative to the aircraft and concludes that its apparent high speed is largely explained by parallax; the office notes uncertainty in some parameters.

Evidence limits

The display does not provide direct target range or a full raw-data stream. Relative motion depends on aircraft altitude, sensor line of sight, and target distance; the visible ocean background can create strong apparent motion. The resolution cannot establish exact object identity from the clip alone.

Conventional alternatives

A slow-moving object such as a balloon carried by wind is consistent with AARO’s assessed motion and environmental context. That interpretation is an evidence-based hypothesis, not a certainty about the object’s precise type. The clip supplies no proof of transmedium or exotic performance.

Calibrated skeptical conclusion

The clip is genuine, but apparent speed is not ground speed. AARO’s parallax explanation substantially weakens the extraordinary-motion claim; exact identification remains limited by missing range and metadata.

What could change this assessment?

Full sensor telemetry, measured range, contemporaneous winds aloft, and original mission data could discriminate a balloon from other slow objects and refine the track.
