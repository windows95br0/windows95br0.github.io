UAP-058 // CASE REVIEW

USS Omaha spherical-object video (2019)

The short clip does not independently document water entry, underwater travel, or an extraordinary vehicle.

The precise claim

A leaked infrared clip filmed from USS Omaha in 2019 appears to show a spherical object entering the water, often described as a craft performing a transmedium maneuver.

Chronology and provenance

The clip was reportedly filmed from USS Omaha off California in July 2019 and circulated online in 2021. DoD confirmed the clip was filmed by Navy personnel and was part of an ongoing review; it did not publicly authenticate the object’s entry into the water or identify it.

Public records and observations

The released video and DoD’s response document an authentic Navy recording. Public versions do not include an uninterrupted, calibrated track, sensor logs, or confirmed underwater contact. Reports of an object “splashing” are interpretations of the image sequence, not an independently corroborated splash or submerged target.

Evidence limits

The camera view is infrared, the target range and size are unknown, and the clip is short and edited. A distant object passing behind the ship’s wake or out of the sensor field can appear to enter the water. No public sonar, radar, or post-event recovery record independently establishes transmedium motion.

Conventional alternatives

A balloon or other airborne object, perspective over the sea, sensor tracking behavior, and disappearance from the field of view are plausible. The evidence does not identify which explanation fits; the absence of an underwater track blocks the stronger claim.

Calibrated skeptical conclusion

Authentic Navy footage is not proof that an object entered the ocean. The transmedium interpretation is unsupported by publicly released corroborating data; no extraterrestrial conclusion follows from an unresolved clip.

What could change this assessment?

Unedited video, synchronized radar and sonar, calibrated range and camera telemetry, and documented recovery or underwater tracking would test whether any object actually crossed the surface.
