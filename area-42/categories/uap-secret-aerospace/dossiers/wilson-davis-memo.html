UAP-038 // CASE REVIEW

Wilson–Davis notes

This unverified secondary artifact does not prove recovered craft; alleged secrecy is not corroboration.

The claim

The exact claim is: A purported 2002 note records Admiral Wilson discussing a hidden reverse-engineering program with physicist Eric Davis. This dossier treats “UAP” as a report category, not a cause. Evidence that an event was reported differs from evidence identifying its stimulus, and both differ from proof of non-human technology.

Timeline & provenance

Provenance and sequence: The text circulated online years later and is attributed to Davis; no signed original or contemporary government file is public. Contemporary records may preserve exact wording better than late recollections, though official files can omit context. Repeated versions of one story are not independent corroboration simply because several people repeat them.

What is independently documented

The documented core is limited: Circulation and public comments are documented, not a verified admission or material evidence. An agency file proves institutional attention, not that every statement in it is true. This distinction matters when a report is incomplete, secondhand, or assembled long after the event.

Physical and sensor limits

The evidence has significant limits: Authorship, date, completeness, and transcription are disputed; even authentic notes report a conversation, not hardware. Without calibrated range, angular size, synchronized clocks, known sensor settings, and raw rather than edited data, estimates of speed, shape, and acceleration may be unreliable.

Mundane and competing explanations

Competing explanations should be tested, not assumed. Misremembered conversation, inaccurate notes, disinformation, or fabrication remain possible without an audit trail. A conventional account must fit timing, direction, environment, and reliable observations. Failure to select one does not make an extraterrestrial explanation the default, especially when basic metadata are missing.

Calibrated status

Assessment: This unverified secondary artifact does not prove recovered craft; alleged secrecy is not corroboration. The conclusion is limited to public evidence. “Unidentified” means that available data did not yield a dependable identification; it is not synonymous with extraterrestrial, interdimensional, or technologically superior. Secrecy alone is not evidence of alien technology.

What would change the view?

Useful evidence would include Authenticated contemporary originals, independent participant accounts, and auditable program records could change assessment. Original files with metadata, independently synchronized observations, reproducible analysis, and transparent custody for physical samples are more valuable than additional retellings.
