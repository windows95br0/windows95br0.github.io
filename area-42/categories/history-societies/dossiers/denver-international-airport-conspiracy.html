FILE A42-HS-083 // HISTORY & SOCIETIES

Denver International Airport and the New World Order Claim

ASSESSMENT: The murals, tunnels and dedication plaque are documented and mundane; no evidence supports a hidden elite bunker or apocalyptic prophecy.

Denver International Airport (DIA), opened in 1995 after a famously delayed, over-budget construction process, has drawn decades of conspiracy claims: that Leo Tanguma's public murals encode a coming genocide, that extensive underground tunnels hide a secret elite bunker (sometimes tied to reptilians or NORAD), and that a dedication capstone bearing a Masonic square-and-compass symbol and the phrase “New World Airport Commission” proves Illuminati or globalist control of the site.

Claim and historical context

The claim rests on three artifacts: two large murals by Leo Tanguma titled “In Peace and Harmony with Nature” and “Children of the World Dream of Peace,” extensive service tunnels beneath the terminal, and a dedication marker naming the “New World Airport Commission.” Conspiracy theorists read the murals' imagery of war, environmental destruction and a girl in a coffin surrounded by grieving figures of different nationalities as a coded prophecy of a coming culling of the population.

Chronology and primary record

DIA opened in February 1995, roughly 16 months late and more than a billion dollars over its original budget, largely due to a failed automated baggage system — a real, well-documented engineering failure that fed public suspicion the airport was built for some undisclosed purpose. The murals were installed as part of a public-art program in the mid-1990s, and the dedication marker was placed at the same time by a local civic group that organized the airport's 1994 dedication ceremonies.

What the record establishes

Leo Tanguma has repeatedly and publicly explained, in interviews with local and national outlets, that his murals depict themes of peace overcoming war, and humanity's collective responsibility to protect children and the environment — conventional public-art themes he has used in murals elsewhere, not a specific prophecy. The airport's extensive tunnels are documented in construction records as serving the (initially failed, later scaled back) automated baggage-handling system and general utility infrastructure, a purpose consistent with any large modern airport's below-ground service space.

Competing interpretations and counterevidence

The “New World Airport Commission” named on the plaque was a real, short-lived local host committee formed to organize the 1994 dedication events, not an international governing body; a nearby Masonic lodge did contribute a time capsule as a civic gesture common to major public building dedications of that era, a tradition with no documented connection to any hidden governing structure. Journalists and researchers who filed public records requests and interviewed involved parties found no undisclosed facility or organization behind either the plaque or the tunnels.

Calibrated verdict

The theory's persistence outlives any single evidence claim because each debunked element (murals, tunnels, plaque) is treated as independently proving a still-hidden truth rather than being resolved; this pattern of moving the goalposts after refutation is a marker distinguishing it from a falsifiable historical claim.

Evidence that could change this assessment

Independently verified architectural plans or leaked internal communications describing a purpose for the tunnels other than baggage handling and utilities, or authenticated testimony from someone with direct knowledge of a hidden facility, would change this assessment. The murals' subjective symbolism and the plaque's ordinary civic wording, without such direct evidence, are not sufficient.
