FILE A42-HS-072 // HISTORY & SOCIETIES

The Secret Protocol of Sèvres

ASSESSMENT: Britain, France, and Israel secretly coordinated the 1956 Suez crisis; the agreement is documented by diplomatic records.

In October 1956, Britain, France, and Israel secretly agreed on a sequence designed to provide a pretext for Anglo-French intervention in Egypt after Israel attacked the Sinai. The Protocol of Sèvres is an authenticated secret agreement, distinct from later speculation about the crisis.

Claim and historical context

The claim is that the three governments planned an apparent mediation crisis to justify British and French military action and regain control around the Suez Canal. Israel would invade Sinai; Britain and France would issue an ultimatum and then intervene.

Chronology and primary record

Israeli, British, and French representatives met at Sèvres on 22–24 October 1956. Israel invaded Egypt on 29 October. Britain and France issued an ultimatum and began military action in early November. U.S. and international pressure forced a ceasefire and withdrawal.

What the record establishes

The signed protocol, participants’ papers, British cabinet records, and later official inquiries establish the agreement. The operation’s sequence closely followed the plan. British Prime Minister Anthony Eden’s public account concealed the prior coordination.

Competing interpretations and counterevidence

The protocol explains the tripartite plan but not every domestic calculation or military action. Individual participants later disputed aspects of intent and responsibility. U.S. opposition, UN action, and financial pressure shaped the outcome, so the secret plan did not guarantee control.

Calibrated verdict

Very high confidence in the secret Anglo-French-Israeli coordination and its use of a manufactured pretext. The evidence documents a specific diplomatic conspiracy, while the crisis’s broader causes and effects require separate analysis.

Evidence that could change this assessment

Additional cabinet files or private correspondence could clarify each government’s internal authorization and post-crisis account. The core verdict would change only if the signed protocol’s provenance were convincingly disproved.
