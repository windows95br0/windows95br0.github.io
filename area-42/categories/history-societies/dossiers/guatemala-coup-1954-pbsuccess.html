FILE A42-HS-066 // HISTORY & SOCIETIES

Guatemala’s 1954 Coup and Operation PBSUCCESS

ASSESSMENT: The CIA covertly backed the operation that toppled President Jacobo Árbenz; its effects and local participation are documented.

The CIA’s Operation PBSUCCESS helped overthrow Guatemalan president Jacobo Árbenz in June 1954. Declassified records show propaganda, political pressure, arms, and a small exile force. The operation’s success depended on Guatemalan military decisions and a wider domestic conflict.

Claim and historical context

The claim is that Washington covertly intervened to remove Árbenz, whose land reform threatened U.S. commercial interests and was portrayed as communist influence. The coup was not simply a spontaneous uprising, nor was the CIA the only actor shaping Guatemala’s political crisis.

Chronology and primary record

Árbenz took office in 1951 and enacted agrarian reform in 1952. The CIA approved PBSUCCESS in 1953. A small force led by Carlos Castillo Armas invaded in June 1954, while radio propaganda and diplomatic pressure amplified the threat. Guatemalan army leaders forced Árbenz to resign on 27 June.

What the record establishes

Declassified CIA plans, operational cables, State Department records, and Guatemalan historical investigations establish the U.S. operation. The files show covert broadcasting, psychological warfare, and efforts to secure military defections. They also reveal planning for repression after the coup.

Competing interpretations and counterevidence

Guatemalan opposition, military calculations, landowner interests, and Cold War politics mattered; reducing the outcome to a U.S. puppet plot obscures domestic actors. The coup’s scale was limited militarily, but its political and human consequences were substantial. Records do not support every later claim about exact U.S. control.

Calibrated verdict

Very high confidence that the CIA engineered and supported a covert intervention decisive to Árbenz’s fall. High confidence in major U.S. responsibility for destabilization. The operation was mediated through Guatemalan elites and institutions, and later violence had multiple perpetrators.

Evidence that could change this assessment

Additional CIA and Guatemalan military files could refine individual orders and the post-coup repression plan. Any revision should weigh the declassified operation documents alongside Guatemalan records and testimony.
