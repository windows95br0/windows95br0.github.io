FILE A42-HS-024 // HISTORY & SOCIETIES

The “Gospel of Jesus’s Wife” Fragment

ASSESSMENT: The fragment is a modern forgery, not evidence of a suppressed marriage record.

A papyrus fragment published in 2012 included the phrase “my wife.” It was promoted as evidence of a hidden early gospel and a church cover-up. Subsequent analysis of its provenance, language, and likely source text showed that it was forged using modern scholarly material.

Claim and historical context

Harvard professor Karen King presented the fragment after receiving it from an anonymous owner. The seller supplied a photocopy of a purported 1999 letter; investigators later traced the owner and related papers to Walter Fritz. The fragment’s acquisition trail was opaque from the beginning.

Chronology and primary record

A 2014 Harvard Theological Review issue published initial arguments and counterarguments. Later work found that the fragment’s Coptic text shared errors with an online transcription of the Gospel of Thomas. A 2016 investigation linked the seller’s documents and wording to modern sources.

What the record establishes

The fragment was never proven to come from an archaeological excavation. The papyrus and ink tests could not date the writing itself conclusively. The decisive evidence came from textual dependence, suspect provenance, and the seller’s documented history rather than a single laboratory result.

Competing interpretations and counterevidence

Some initial tests found the papyrus old, but ancient blank papyrus can be used for modern forgery. That distinction explains why material age alone did not authenticate the text. No authenticated ancient manuscript corroborates the phrase or a church effort to suppress it.

Calibrated verdict

The fragment is widely regarded as a modern forgery. The episode documents how an anonymous provenance claim and media anticipation can outrun authentication. It does not establish whether Jesus was married; it establishes that this fragment cannot answer the question.

Evidence that could change this assessment

An excavation record predating modern publication, a securely documented chain of custody, and independent textual witnesses could alter the case. Further testing of the writing materials may refine the method, but age of papyrus alone would not reverse the verdict.
