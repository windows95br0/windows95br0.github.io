FILE A42-HS-001 // HISTORY & SOCIETIES

The Protocols of the Elders of Zion

ASSESSMENT: Fabricated antisemitic propaganda; no authentic conspiracy record.

The text alleges a secret Jewish program for world domination. It is a forged political tract, not a leaked record. Its documented story concerns authorship, plagiarism, publication, and state-backed circulation—and the harm caused when fabricated evidence was presented as proof.

Claim and historical context

The Russian-language text appeared in installments in 1903 and as a book in 1905, amid imperial crisis and organized antisemitism. Its author remains uncertain; that uncertainty does not make its claims credible. Investigators traced extensive passages to Maurice Joly’s 1864 political satire attacking Napoleon III.

Chronology and primary record

The most probative comparison is textual: passages in the Protocols reproduce Joly’s dialogue, changing the targets while retaining distinctive wording and sequence. The 1921 Times of London published parallel excerpts; later scholarship places the forgery in the milieu of Russian far-right propaganda.

What the record establishes

A 1935 Swiss court found the text plagiarized and defamatory in a Bern trial. Nazi publishers later disseminated it, and it remains in circulation. These are records of fabrication and propaganda, not evidence that the alleged meetings or plan ever existed.

Competing interpretations and counterevidence

Some defenders claim independent corroboration or insist the text is a warning rather than a document. No authenticated minutes, participants, operational records, or independent evidence substantiate its alleged council. The text’s antisemitic premises and copied source explain its content better than a hidden organization.

Calibrated verdict

The documented conspiracy is the effort to pass a forgery as evidence and mobilize hostility against Jews. Scholarly consensus rejects the alleged plot. Repetition of the text without its provenance launders propaganda; a quotation is not an evidentiary endorsement.

Evidence that could change this assessment

A materially different assessment would require authenticated contemporary records, independently verified provenance, and corroboration unrelated to the forged text. No such evidence is known. New archival findings about the forger could clarify production, but could not retroactively validate the fictitious conspiracy.
